# PulseTrack 💪

**A free workout tracker that lives on your phone and works with no internet.**
Pick a program, log your sets, beat your records. No account, no ads, no sign-up. Your data stays on your phone (the optional live family board, off until you start or join one, sends only a small scrambled update).

*Version 2.98.0*

PulseTrack installs to your home screen like a normal app. It comes with **59 ready-made programs** (nearly all of them planned over several weeks, six of them two-a-day, with morning and evening sessions; 12-week plans from Arnold, Ronnie Coleman, Phil Heath and Kris Gethin, plus routines of pro bodybuilders like Mike Mentzer, Dorian Yates, Frank Zane, Chris Bumstead, Flex Wheeler, Lee Priest, Layne Norton, Reg Park, Dave Draper and Dexter Jackson) and **646 exercises**, and you can add your own of both. It has rest and interval timers, a separate **cardio and sports logger**, charts, personal records, a 1-rep-max calculator, body tracking, and a muscle map that shows what is recovered and what is behind. You can change how it looks (light or dark, 304 themes, any colour, 40 fonts).

## Install and update

**Install.** Open the app link in your phone's browser once, with internet.

| Phone | Steps |
|---|---|
| iPhone (Safari) | Share button → **Add to Home Screen** → Add |
| Android (Chrome) | ⋮ menu → **Install app** (or **Add to Home screen**) |

After that it opens full screen like any app and works offline. **Settings → App** shows **Works offline ✓** when the whole app is saved on the phone (and the side menu shows "offline ✓" next to the version). If it ever says **Not saved for offline yet**, tap **Save now** with internet on.

**Update.** When new files have been uploaded to GitHub:

1. Open the app **with internet**.
2. Close it fully and open it again.
3. You have the new version, and your data is still there.

Android updates on the first refresh. iPhone can be slower: if you don't see the update, close the app completely and reopen it while online.

## Your first workout

1. Open the app. You land on **Home**. Nothing is picked for you.
2. Tap **Choose a Program** and look around. Opening a program only lets you look, it never makes it yours. Tap **Select** on the one you want (more in [Your current program](#your-current-program)), or start **Freestyle** for a free workout.
3. Tap a day to see every exercise with its sets, reps and rest.
4. For each set, type your **weight** and **reps**, then tap the check mark. The rest timer starts by itself.
5. Tap **Finish Workout**. You get a summary, and the next day lines up as **Up next**.

### The Programs screen (new look in 2.37.0, two-a-day shelf in 2.40.0)

The list used to be a tall stack of buttons and big cards. It is now built to be scanned:

- **Current program card** on top: the program you are on, your week, and the next workout (**Next: Week 3 · Day 2: Pull**), with an **Open** button. If nothing is selected it says so.
- **Two-a-day shelf (new in 2.40.0):** right under the current program, a row of sunrise-to-night cards, one per two-a-day program. Each shows the athlete, the title, the **AM** session and the **PM** session of its first double day (for example **AM Chest & Back / PM Thighs, Calves & Abs**), and days a week. Swipe sideways for the rest, tap a card to open the program, or tap **See all**. The shelf only shows on the plain All view; search or any filter hides it so the list stays the list.
- **Tools in one compact grid:** **Build me a program** across the top, then **Create your own**, **Paste a program**, **Import a file**, **Share link**, **Guided mobility** and **Stretch routine**, and (new in 2.43.0) **Steal a day** and **Playlist** on a row of their own. Same buttons as before, a third of the height.
- **Search** by program name, athlete, tag, workout name or **exercise**. Typing "hack squat" lists every program that has one; "zane" lists his three; "mentzer pullover" narrows to the one program that has both. Every word has to match, and the match is highlighted.
- **Filter chips with counts:** **All**, **Pro plans** (tied to a named athlete), **Two-a-day** (any program with morning and evening sessions), **Mass**, **Cutting**, **Strength**, **Beginner** and **Mine** (programs you made or imported).
- **One slim row per program:** a coloured badge with the athlete's initials (the same colour for every program of one athlete), the title, and one line of facts, like **Mass · 6 days/wk · 12 weeks · Advanced**. Two-a-day programs also carry a small gold **☀ ☾ AM+PM** tag (**☀ wk 10–12** when only some weeks train twice a day). Rows are grouped as **Your programs**, **Pro programs** (by athlete) and **More programs**. The current program's row says **✓ Current**.
- **Tap a row to open it. Tap ⋯ for the rest:** **Open program**, **Steal a day from it** (2.43.0), **Make it my current program**, **Add to my calendar** (2.68.0), **Share** and **Delete from this phone** (asks first in the app's own dialog, then offers **Undo** for 10 seconds; a built-in program can also be added back later).
- The labels (athlete, goal, level, days a week) come from `programs.js`. Programs you made yourself get a guess from their title and tags.

#### How a two-a-day program looks inside (new in 2.40.0)

Open one and the day list shows what each session is. A day titled **Mon AM: Chest & Back** reads **☀ MONDAY · MORNING** over **Chest & Back**, with a gold bar down the left; **Mon PM: Thighs, Calves & Abs** reads **☾ MONDAY · EVENING** with an indigo bar and sits tight under it, so each day's pair is one block. A note above the list says how it runs: do the morning session, eat and rest, then the evening session a few hours later. Each session is still its own workout, with its own sets, timer and finish screen, and it counts when you finish it, so **Up next** goes from Monday morning to Monday evening to Tuesday. The progress line says **sessions done** instead of days. Programs that put both in one day (Franco Columbu's **Chest, Shoulders, Abs (AM) + Arms (PM)**) show the two sessions on two lines inside the card.

It works for your own programs too: name the days **Mon AM: Legs** and **Mon PM: Arms** (or **Legs (AM) + Arms (PM)**) and they get the same look, the AM+PM tag, and a place on the Two-a-day shelf.

### Steal a day (new in 2.43.0)

Take one workout from any program without taking the whole program. **Programs → Steal a day** opens a finder:

- **Pick a muscle** (Chest, Back, Shoulders, Arms, Quads, Hams & glutes, Calves, Abs) and it lists the best days for it from every program in the library plus your own, best first. A day is ranked by how many of its hard sets hit that muscle, and only days that are mostly that muscle (at least 40% of the sets and at least 5 sets) are listed. Each row shows the day, the athlete and program, its biggest muscle groups with set counts, and the number of exercises and minutes.
- **Or search** an athlete, a program or a day name ("Cutler", "back width", "PHAT"). The list shows 30 at a time with **Show more**.
- **Tap a day** to see all its exercises with sets and reps, then choose what to do with it:
  - **Do it now:** a one-off **Freestyle** workout with those exercises. Your program is not touched, and your current program stays your current program. If your Freestyle workout already has sets logged, it asks before replacing it.
  - **Add to my current program:** a new day at the end of it. Only offered when your current program is one you made yourself (see below).
  - **Add to one of my programs:** pick any program you made, and the day goes at the end of it.
  - **Start a new program from it:** a program of your own with just that day. Add more days to it later from the same screen.
- **Nothing in a built-in program is ever edited.** The day is copied with fresh ids, and supersets are kept (the last exercise is never left linked to nothing). That is why **Add to my current program** is not offered while a built-in program is current: use **Add to one of my programs** or **Start a new program from it** instead.
- **Other ways in:** a program's **⋯** menu (**Steal a day from it**, showing only that program's days), the workout screen's **⋯ More → Steal this day…** (for the day on screen), and the **Weak points** card on Progress → Strength, whose **Find a day for triceps** button (it names your weakest muscle) opens the finder already set to the matching muscle.

### Program playlist (new in 2.43.0)

Run programs one after another with no thinking about it, for example **8 weeks of Chris Bumstead's push / pull / legs, 1 deload week, then 8 weeks of Arnold's double split**. **Programs → Playlist**:

- **Build a playlist:** add programs (search by name or athlete) and **deload weeks**, set the number of weeks for each with − and +, reorder with the arrows, remove with ✕. It shows the total, for example "17 weeks in all, about 4 months". A program you add runs for its own length (12 weeks for most of the library). Two ready-made ones are offered: **Mass cycle** (12 weeks of CBum push/pull/legs, a week off, 12 weeks of Arnold's double split) and **Base then size** (6 weeks of Reg Park's full-body 5x5, a deload week, 12 weeks of Frank Zane's upper / lower).
- **Start today** makes the first program your current program, starting at **Day 1** (its earlier progress in the program is cleared, and the app tells you so before you start).
- **It moves on by itself.** Each time you open the app, if the current stage's weeks are up, the next stage begins: the next program becomes your current program at Day 1 and a note says so. A deload stage keeps your current program and tells you to run it with the same weights and about half the sets. It only ever moves **one stage at a time**, so a long break never skips a whole program. After the last stage it says **Playlist finished**.
- **Home shows one slim row** above the work check-in: **Week 2 of 8 · Chris Bumstead: Push / Pull / Legs** and **Next: Deload week on Dec 23**. Tap it for the whole line-up with dates, a ✓ on finished stages and the current one highlighted. There you can **Skip to next stage**, add **+1 week** to the current stage, or **Stop playlist** (your current program stays as it is). If you switch to a different program on your own, the sheet offers **Make it my current program** to get back on track.
- **On Progress too:** the **History** tab's **Training year** section shows the whole chain with dates above the calendar (current stage marked, later stages with their start and end dates).
- If a program in the playlist was deleted from the phone, it is added back from the built-in list when its turn comes.
- The playlist is saved with your settings, so it is in backups. Stopping it, or never starting one, changes nothing.

### Your current program

Your **current program** is the one that Home, the side menu and **Start Workout** use. **You** choose it. The app never picks it for you, and browsing never changes it.

- On the **Programs** screen, tap **⋯** on a row and choose **Make it my current program**, or open a program and tap **Select**. The one you picked sits in the **Current program** card at the top, and its row shows a green **✓ Current** tag.
- Open a program you haven't selected and a bar says **Just looking** with a **Select** button. Inside one of its workouts, a small bar says **Not your current program** with **Select it**, which also makes that day your next one.
- While you look around, Home and the side menu keep showing your own program.
- **Switch week** counts as choosing the program, because you are starting it there.
- **Empty Workout**, **Do Again**, **Freestyle**, and the **Open** buttons after you add or create a program do not change it.
- If nothing is selected and you finish a program's workout, a note offers **Make "…" your current program?**. Nothing happens unless you tap it.
- Deleting a program you were only looking at leaves your current one alone.

### Programs that run for several weeks

- A **week bar** is pinned right under the header of the Days screen (**Week 4 of 12 · 3/64 done**, with a **Switch week** button). It stays there while you scroll the list of days.
- **Switch week** opens a grid of the weeks. Tap the one you are on: earlier weeks are marked done and **Up next** jumps there. Going back to an earlier week works the same way. Your saved workouts and PRs are never changed.
- This works for every program that is laid out week by week: the 12-week plans, Kris Gethin 12 Weeks to Muscle and Legacy 12-Week, FST-7 Big and Ripped, both Jim Stoppani 6-week programs, the programs that repeat a few days over several weeks (Kai Greene 6 weeks: days 1-6 for weeks 1-3, days 7-12 for weeks 4-6; FST-7 Level 1: weeks 1 and 3 use days 1-5, weeks 2 and 4 use days 6-10), and, since 2.48.0, almost everything else (below). For those, the plan stays on the first set of days until you finish them, then moves to the next week.
- **Longer programs (new in 2.48.0).** The programs that used to be one week on repeat now run for several weeks, so the week bar counts a real plan (**Week 6 of 12 · Build**) and the days list has headers like **Weeks 5-8 · Build**. Which weeks are real and which are ours is spelled out in each program's description:
  - **Weeks as published.** Shred with Buendia is 8 weeks (Phase 1 is weeks 1-4, Phase 2 is weeks 5-8). Arnold's Blueprint is one 8-week program: Phase 1 (from his Blueprint to Mass) is weeks 1-4 and Phase 2 (from his Blueprint to Cut) is weeks 5-8, six days a week each (in Phase 2 Monday/Thursday, Tuesday/Friday and Wednesday/Saturday repeat). Frank Zane's beginner routine is 8 weeks: one set of every exercise for weeks 1-3, then two sets, as his manual says.
  - **Weeks that PulseTrack adds.** The original sources only describe one week or a few weeks, so for the rest we added a plain progression and say so in the description. In the 12-week programs, weeks 1-4 are the routine exactly as published. **Build** (weeks 5-8) adds a set to the first two lifts of each day (never above 5 sets) and two reps to the other plain lifts. **Push** (weeks 9-11) goes back to the published sets but heavier on the first two lifts (two fewer reps, 20 s more rest). **Deload** (week 12) is about 60% of the sets, so go lighter. Jay Cutler's 4-day split is 8 weeks the same way (4 as published, 3 Build, 1 Deload).
  - **Method programs are left alone.** Layne Norton's PHAT, Kris Gethin's DTP split, Super Shredded 8, Vince Gironda's 8x8 (6 weeks plus an easy week) and 10-8-6-15 (8 weeks) keep every set and rep as published, with just an easy final week. Arnold's Golden Six is the same three workouts for 12 weeks (the length is our choice), adding weight whenever you hit all the reps. Anything that is part of a method (8x8, FST-7 finishers, pyramids like 10, 8, 6, 15, drop sets, Tabata, AMRAP) is never changed in any program.
  - A day that would come out the same in a later phase is simply reused, so the days list only shows what really changes. Progress you already had is kept: a "Week 3" counter becomes week 3 of the plan.
- The **Pro Leg Days** and **Pro Upper-Body Days** collections, and programs you build or import yourself, are still a single week repeated. They have the same bar with a plain week counter, and **Switch week** asks for the week number.
- Every day is labelled like **Week 4 of 12 · Day 2**, the days list has week headers and scrolls to **Up next**, and rest days are skipped automatically.
- **Switch week** counts as choosing the program, because you are starting it there.
- History remembers which week each workout was in.

### Add training to your calendar (new in 2.68.0)

Put your program's workouts in your phone's calendar, so they show up next to everything else, with a reminder. Tap **⋯** on a program in the Programs list, then **Add to my calendar**.

- **One small sheet.** **First workout** (a date), **Time** (6:00 PM to start), **Remind me** (none, at the start, 15 minutes, 30 minutes or 1 hour before; 30 minutes to start), **For** how many weeks, and **Training days** (Mon to Sun buttons). Under it a box says how many workouts that makes, the first and last date, and the first three. **Add to calendar** saves a small calendar file (`.ics`). Open it, or choose Calendar from the share list if your phone offers one, and your calendar adds every workout.
- **One event per workout.** The title is the day's name (**Chest, Upper Back & Abs**, without the "Monday:" or "Week 3" in front), the notes say the program, the week and the day, then list the exercises with sets and reps. Each event is as long as the day's estimated minutes.
- **Which days.** A program that names its weekdays (**Monday: Chest**, **Mon AM: Arms**) keeps those weekdays, and the Mon to Sun buttons are hidden because the program already decided. Any other program goes on the days you pick, and the usual days for its number of workouts a week are already picked (three workouts: Mon, Wed, Fri; four: Mon, Tue, Thu, Fri; five: Mon to Fri; six: Mon to Sat). Rest days in a program are not events: the weekdays are the rest days. If the program has 6 workouts a week and you pick 4 days, the box says its weeks will take a little longer.
- **Weeks.** A program with weeks (**Week 3 of 8**, a week plan) goes week by week and starts with all of its weeks; **−** and **＋** change how many. A program that repeats one week repeats it for the number of weeks in its name ("6-Weeks to Massive Arms" gives 6), or 4 if the name has none, up to 12.
- **Morning and evening.** On a two-a-day program the **AM** session goes at 7:00 AM (or earlier, if you picked an earlier time) and the **PM** session at 5:00 PM (or later), both on the same day.
- **Safe to do again.** Every workout has a fixed ID, so adding the same program again after you changed the time or the days updates those events in most calendar apps instead of doubling them. Events that no longer exist (you chose fewer weeks) stay until you delete them in the calendar.
- **Times do not move.** They use your phone's own clock, so a 6:00 PM workout stays at 6:00 PM when you travel.
- **Remembered.** The time, the reminder and the days you picked are saved in your settings (and in a backup) for next time.
- Freestyle has no set days, so it has nothing to add. This is separate from **Phone alerts** on timers and the pre-workout protocol, which make their own small calendar files.

### Skipping a workout

Can't train today (a long day of work, an injury, a trip)? You don't need to delete the exercises or turn the day into a rest day.

- On the Home card tap **Skip this workout**, or tap **Skip day** at the top of the workout screen. You get one question first: **Skip this workout?**
- The day counts as done, so **Up next** moves on to the following day (and to the next week when the week is finished), the same as after finishing a workout. It works the same in the week-by-week plans and in the programs that repeat a few days.
- **Nothing is saved to History.** No volume, stats or streak days are added, and your records are never touched.
- Any sets you had already checked off for that day are cleared, so it comes back fresh. The question tells you how many.
- In the days list a skipped day says **SKIPPED** instead of **DONE**. It stays in the program, and you can still open it and do it later; finishing it makes it a normal **DONE** day.
- **Undo** stays on the screen for a few seconds after skipping and puts everything back exactly as it was.
- The buttons only show for the program you are currently on, on a day that has exercises. Rest days have their own **mark as done**, and Freestyle workouts have nothing to skip.

### Busy-season version (new in 2.35.0)

Slammed at work? Open any program's day list and tap **Busy-season version**. Pick **2, 3 or 4 days a week** and **30, 45 or 60 minutes** a workout. The app builds full-body days from the exercises that program leans on most (a squat, a hinge, a push, a pull and so on, at least three muscle groups a day) and checks each day fits your time. The preview shows the days before you save; **Create this program** adds it as a new program called "... · Busy season". Your own program is not changed, so you can switch back when the busy stretch ends. The button is hidden on Freestyle and on busy-season programs themselves.

### Build me a program, pick your own muscles (new in 2.50.0)

On the Programs screen, **Build me a program** now has a fifth split next to Push / Pull / Legs, Upper / Lower and the rest: **Pick my own muscles**. You build the week, the app fills in the exercises.

- **Your days.** Each day is a card. Tap the muscles that day trains (chest, back, shoulders, biceps, triceps, forearms, quads, hamstrings, glutes, calves, abs and core, traps, lower back) and set **how many exercises each muscle gets**, 1 to 8. Or start a day from a ready-made one (Push, Pull, Legs, Upper, Lower, Chest & back, Shoulders & arms, Arms, Core). Up to 14 days.
- **Rest days are days in the list.** Tap **＋ Rest day** and put it where you want it, so "rest only on Sunday" is a 7-day list with the last day a rest day.
- **Copy a day.** **Copy** adds a day that runs the very same exercises ("Same exercises as day 1"). **Make it different** turns the copy into its own day with its own picks. Removing the original hands its muscles to the copy.
- **Supersets.** Tick **Chest ↔ back** (Arnold style: one chest exercise, one back exercise, over and over), **Biceps ↔ triceps**, **Quads ↔ hamstrings** and **Abs in pairs** (abs run two by two, for example leg raises with bent-knee raises). With none ticked every exercise stands alone. The longer list starts so the alternation lasts the whole day.
- **Exercise choice.** **Classics** (default: the well-known lifts and what you already do), **Balanced** or **Mix it up** (fresher picks, steering away from what you did lately). Goal and equipment work as before. Machines only, dumbbells only or bodyweight only builds what fits and says so when a muscle runs short.
- **Preview before it is saved.** Every day is listed with its exercises, sets, reps and superset pairs. **↻** swaps one for another that trains the same muscle (the superset pair stays), **✕** removes one (its partner is freed), **Shuffle** re-picks one day with the same muscle counts, **Shuffle everything** re-picks the lot, **Change the days** goes back, **Save program** saves it. Copied days follow the day they copy.
- **Length and name.** 1 week (repeat it) or 4 weeks with progression (week 2 one more rep, week 3 one more set on the big lifts, week 4 an easy week; rest days stay rest days). Give it a name; it is saved under **More programs** with the Custom tag.
- Your days, supersets, exercise choice and name are remembered for the next time.

### Make your own

- **Program builder:** create a program, add days and exercises, set sets, reps and rest.
- **Edit any program:** add, remove, swap or reorder exercises, and change sets and reps.
- **Move exercises up and down:** every exercise card on the workout screen has an up arrow and a down arrow (top right, above Swap and Delete). Tap one and the exercise changes places with the one above or below it. The card stays under your thumb, so you can keep tapping to carry it further. The arrows are greyed out at the top and bottom of the list. Sets you already typed or checked off move with their exercise. A superset (A1/A2) moves as one block, so it never gets split up; to change the order inside a superset, unlink it, move the exercise, then link it again. It works in every program, including Freestyle, and the new order is remembered. **Clear Progress** puts the program's original order back.
- **Save a finished workout as a routine** to repeat it.
- **Supersets:** two exercises done back to back. They show as A1/A2, B1/B2 and each has its own weight and reps. To make one, tap **+ Superset with next** in the gap between two exercises; tap **Superset with next · Unlink** to separate them. The rest timer waits until the last exercise of the pair.
- **Share** a program as a link, or **import** one from a link or a `.json` file.
- **Share one workout day (new in 2.19.0).** Edit a day the way you like it, then tap **Share day** on the workout screen (next to Clear Progress). It sends that day exactly as it is now, with your extra sets and swapped exercises, as a link (or a small file if it is very big). The other person opens the link, or taps **Programs → Paste a Share Link** and pastes it. A sheet shows what is inside and where it will go: by default the **same day of the same program** if they have it, otherwise the next day of their current program. They can change the program and the day, then tap **Replace Day N**. That day's exercises, name and length are swapped for yours. Their own day is kept underneath like any other edit, so **Clear Progress** on that day puts **their original day back** (exercises, name and length), and so does **Reset All Days**. A note under the day's name says it is a shared workout until then. Sets they had logged on that day are cleared when it is replaced; saved workouts in History and records are never touched. Or they can tap **Just do it once (Freestyle)** to run the workout without changing any program. On an iPhone, a link opened in Safari does not see the app on the Home Screen (they keep separate data), so the app offers to copy the link: open the Home Screen icon and use **Programs → Paste a Share Link**.

### Reset and restore

- **Clear Progress** puts a day back to exactly the program's original: added exercises and extra sets go, removed ones come back. Your History and records are never deleted.
- **Deleted a built-in program?** **Add Back a Built-in Program** restores it.

## While you train

### Logging a set

- **Set tags:** **R** regular, **W** warm-up, **D** drop set, **F** failure.
- **Previous numbers:** each set shows what you did last time.
- **Dumbbells:** enter the weight of **one** dumbbell. Your volume counts both hands. Since 2.60.0 that is true for **every** dumbbell exercise, hammer curls, lateral and front raises, rear delt flyes, flyes and kickbacks included (before, only exercises with "dumbbell" in the name counted two). One-dumbbell moves (goblet squat, pullover, overhead extension, side bend, single-arm and one-leg moves) still count one, and so do your own exercises, which keep the kind you picked. **Settings → Count both dumbbells** turns it off.
- **Typo check (new in 2.60.0):** when you tap the check on a set that looks like a slip of the finger, a small sheet asks before it is saved, for example **Did you mean 70?** (you typed 700) with **Use 70**, **Keep 700** and **Let me change it**. It compares with what you did last time on that exercise and only speaks up when a number is far off: a weight about 5 times higher or lower than last time (an extra or missing zero), 30 or more reps at 4 times last time, or weight and reps typed in each other's boxes. When it cannot guess the fix it asks **Is that weight right?**. It does not ask about warm-up or drop sets, bodyweight moves, cardio, exercises with no earlier numbers, or an ordinary jump up, and **Keep** means that same number on that exercise is not asked about again this session. Works in Guided mode too. **Settings → Typo check** turns it off.
- **Each side or total? (new in 2.38.0):** cable, machine and dumbbell exercises have a small tappable label above the weight box. A cable crossover has two stacks, a plate machine has plates on both sides, so do you type 25 or 50? Tap the label and pick **Each side** (type one stack, one side or one dumbbell, and volume counts both) or **Total** (type everything together, and volume counts it once). The label always says which one the app expects: **Each side · Reps**, **Total · Reps** or, for dumbbells, **Each hand · Reps**. Every cable and machine exercise that can have two stacks or plates on both sides (seated cable rows, pulldowns, cable curls and pushdowns, chest, row and shoulder machines, leg presses and so on; since 2.60.3, before that only crossovers, flies and cable presses) shows an amber **Each stack or total?** until you choose. It stays quiet on one-handle and one-leg moves (single-arm, single-leg) and on moves that are one load by nature (leg extensions and curls, calf raises, hip abductors, kickbacks, ab and woodchopper moves, pec decks, shrugs). The choice is remembered per exercise (and travels in backups), and the same note shows in Guided mode next to the weight. **25 each side and 50 total count as the same work** (500 volume at 10 reps either way). It only affects workouts you save from now on; past sessions keep how they were logged. Estimated 1RM, PRs, volume and History show the number you typed, and (since 2.62.0) **strength levels match it to the standard**: see *Strength levels follow your choices* below. **Back to the app's default** in the same sheet undoes it. Dumbbell exercises still start as Each hand, and barbell, bodyweight and cardio exercises have no switch.
- **Change it from the exercise (new in 2.64.0):** tap an exercise anywhere (Progress, records, search, History) and its sheet has a small **How you type it** card, above the exercise note: **Weight** (**Each side**, or **Each hand** on dumbbells, / **Total**) and, where the exercise has the choice, **Reps** (**Per hand**, **Per leg** or **Per side** / **Total**). It is the same choice as the label in a workout, so new sets use it straight away. It says **auto** until you pick one. Right after you change it, if older workouts of that exercise are counted the other way, the card says how many and offers **Change them too**: those sets get your new answer, their volume is worked out again, and records and strength levels follow. **Undo** puts them back. Nothing about past workouts changes unless you tap it. Barbell, bodyweight and cardio exercises have no card.
- **Reps: per hand or total? (new in 2.59.0, on every dumbbell exercise since 2.59.1):** every dumbbell exercise (hammer curls, lateral and front raises, flyes, kickbacks, presses, rows, curls, lunges and step-ups, and so on, whether or not "dumbbell" is in the name), the one-sided cable and machine moves (single-arm, single-leg, unilateral) and iso-lateral machines get a second small button right next to the weight one. Tap **Reps: per hand** (or **per leg**, or **per side**) when you type the reps of ONE hand (10 on each arm: type 10, and volume counts both hands), or **Reps: total** when you add both together (10 on each arm: type 20, and volume counts every rep once). The button always says how the app is counting right now, shown quietly until you pick; picking one highlights it. The choice is remembered per exercise (and travels in backups), shows in Guided mode ("each hand · reps total") and is saved with every set, so volume, estimated 1RM, PRs, strength levels and History all read your reps the way you meant them: a set of 20 total reps is graded like 10 per hand, and History shows it as **30 × 20 total**. With two dumbbells in a lunge or step-up, **per leg** counts both dumbbells on every step of both legs. It works together with the weight button (each hand/total weight and per hand/total reps can be mixed freely). It is not offered where there is nothing to split: goblet squats, pullovers, overhead extensions and leg curls (one dumbbell held with both hands), and barbell, bodyweight and cardio exercises. Past sessions keep how they were logged, and **Back to the app's default** in the same sheet undoes it.
- **Bodyweight moves:** pull-ups and dips use your body weight, with a **Weighted / Assisted** switch. The same idea works on every other bodyweight exercise (see the next item).
- **Bodyweight exercises: 0 is your body, a number is extra (new in 2.20.0, extra weight added in 2.54.0):** on push-ups, sit-ups (plain, decline, weighted and weighted decline are all the same exercise: type 0 for your body or the weight you hold), Russian twists, planks, hanging leg raises, back extensions, bench dips, bodyweight squats and the rest of the Library's **Bodyweight** exercises, **0** means just your body and a number is **extra weight on top**: with 180 lb logged, **0** counts 180 lb, **30** counts 180 + 30 = 210 lb. (Before 2.54.0 a 30 counted as only 30 lb, so adding weight could make a set worth less than none.) The box says **BW** for a 0 and **+lbs** for anything else, the column says **Added · Reps**, and a one-line note shows the sum as you type. Volume, estimated 1RM, PRs, charts, the **e1RM** chip and strength levels use it; History shows **BW × 12** or **BW+30 × 12**. An **empty** box still counts as nothing, so only a typed number counts. Other exercises (dumbbells, cables, machines, barbells) are unchanged: a 30 there is 30. Each set remembers your body weight that day, so a new body weight only changes sets logged without one. Sets from before 2.54.0 are read the same way (and their totals worked out again once), except that a number of **half your body weight or more** is treated as the whole load, as before, since it was most likely the total. If no body weight is logged yet, a note under the exercise offers to open **Body & Photos**. When you hit the top of the rep range on every set, the suggestion says to add reps or hold a weight.
- **Timed sets (per-set timers new in 2.42.0):** planks, holds, hangs and carries (and any set with a target like "45s" or "40 sec") get a ⏱ button on the set. It counts up, or down from the target, and beeps. If the reps are written one entry per set, like **8-10, 8-10, 40 sec, 40 sec**, each set reads its own entry: only the two 40-second sets get a timer, and the reps box on each set shows that set's own target. On a **weighted** timed set (leg press for 40 seconds, curls for 40 seconds) the seconds are **not** written into the reps box, because they would make a fake estimated 1RM; the timer saves the seconds, tells you the time is up, and you type the reps you got. Bodyweight holds like planks still fill the reps box with the seconds.
- **Cardio:** log minutes and distance, and see your pace over time.
- **Notes:** add a note to any exercise or day.
- **Swap an exercise:** tap swap and **★ Similar exercises** lists the closest matches first, each with a short reason ("Same movement · Cable · Reverse grip"). **Everything in the list trains the same muscle** as the exercise you are looking at (the muscle is shown next to the title, for example "Side Delts · closest first"). Chest Dips only suggests chest exercises, Triceps Dips only triceps, and a barbell row never suggests a shoulder exercise. Inside that muscle the order comes from the movement, the equipment and the grip or angle, so Rope Pushdown suggests other pushdowns before it suggests a triceps machine, and a bench press suggests other flat presses before incline ones. Exercises you have done before are marked with your last weight and reps. Below the list, every exercise in that muscle group is still there.
- **Search by any name:** the exercise picker and the Library also search an exercise's other names. Type "barbell bench press" or "triceps rope pushdown" and you get Bench Press or Rope Pushdown, with "also called …" under it.
- **Form tips:** each exercise card has a **Form tips ›** link with a short checklist written for that exact exercise (a one-line **What it is** for the machines added in 2.61.0, then the set-up and the movement step by step, and the usual mistake to avoid) and a button to watch a video on YouTube. Every exercise in the library has its own, and every exercise the built-in programs use is in the library too. Exercises you create yourself use a general set of tips when one fits, since they have none of their own.
- **Form clips (new in 2.44.0):** next to **Form tips ›** each exercise card has a **🎥 Clips** button (with the count when there are some). It opens that exercise's clips and lets you **Record a clip** with the phone's camera or **Choose a video** you already filmed. The video file is saved as it is (nothing is re-encoded or uploaded); a clip filmed from an exercise card remembers the heaviest set you logged that day, so two clips can read **205 × 5** and **225 × 5**. Tap a clip to watch it with **0.25×, 0.5× and 1×** speed, **◀ frame / frame ▶** to step one frame at a time, a scrubber, an optional note, **Share** and **Delete**. **Compare two clips** plays two of them side by side with one set of controls (the scrubber follows the longer one). **⋯ More → Form clips** and **Body → Form clips** open the whole collection, grouped by exercise. Clips live on this phone only, like progress photos: **they are not in the backup file** (too big) and **Reset everything** removes them. A clip over 120 MB asks before saving and one over 400 MB is refused; film 10 to 20 seconds of one set. Some phones will not play a video in a format they cannot decode, in which case the clip saves but its picture stays blank.
- **Bar path and bar speed (new in 2.46.0):** on barbell lifts (squats, bench, deadlifts, presses, rows, cleans and the like) a **🎯 Bar path** button sits next to **🎥 Clips**. Prop the phone side-on so the plate on the end of the bar stays in the picture, then **tap the left edge of the plate and the right edge**. Choose which plate it is at the top (a 45 lb or 20 kg plate is 450 mm across; 35, 25, 10 and 5 lb plates and a custom size are in the list): the plate's real size gives the scale, so speeds come out in metres a second. A green ring follows the plate straight away, so you can check it holds before you lift. Press the red button (with an optional 5 or 10 second delay to get under the bar), lift, and press it again. The path is drawn on screen as you go and the clip is saved with the exercise. A clip you filmed earlier, or picked from your library, works too: **Form clips → open the clip → 🎯 Track bar path**, scrub to a frame where the plate is clear, tap its edges and press **Track**. **Results:** the path over the first frame (slow parts red, fast parts green, a numbered dot at the top of each rep), the **average speed**, the **best rep**, the **velocity loss** (how much slower the last rep was than the fastest) and the range of motion, then a row per rep with **up speed, peak speed, time up and front-to-back drift**, and a plain line on what the set looked like. **Share picture** makes a card of it. The numbers are saved with the clip and shown under it in the player (**View**, **Track again**). It all happens on the phone: a small tracker written for this app follows the plate frame by frame, so nothing is uploaded and no model has to be downloaded; it works offline. **Tips for good numbers:** film from the side at the height of the plate, keep the phone steady (lean it on something), use even light, and film every set from the same spot so sets can be compared. Phone cameras blur fast bars a little, so compare your own sets filmed the same way rather than other people's numbers. The tracker can lose the plate if something covers it, if it swings a long way out of the picture or if the picture is very dark; it says so and the numbers are marked as rough. A clip recorded inside the app is saved as a WebM or MP4 file, whichever the phone's browser makes.
- **Warm-up generator:** builds warm-up sets for your first lift.
- **Screen stays awake** during a workout, on phones that allow it.
- **Resume:** if you leave the app mid-workout, it picks up where you stopped.

### Say a set (rebuilt in 2.37.0)

The **🎤 Say a set** button on the workout screen fills in a set from your voice. The first version often missed, so it was rebuilt.

- **It understands how people talk in a gym:** "bench 185 for 8", "one eighty five for eight", "two plates for five", "8 reps at 135", "225 x 5", "pull ups 12", "curl forty five pounds ten reps". Phones often write "for" as "4", so "185 4 8" is read as 185 for 8.
- **It never fills a set blind.** A small panel shows what it heard, with the exercise, weight and reps ready to fix and a **Log set** button. Pick a different exercise or change a number, then log.
- **If the phone can't listen** (no internet, microphone blocked, or the iPhone Home Screen app, which does not allow speech input) it says why and lets you type the set in the same panel.
- **Settings → Sound, Voice & Feel → Say a set:** **Ask first** (default), **Log right away** (logs it and shows **Undo**), or **Off** (hides the button).
- **Limits:** most phones send your voice to the phone maker for recognition, so it needs internet and can fail in a basement gym. That is a phone limit, not something the app can fix while staying offline.

### In the gym (new in 2.8.0)

Each of these can be switched off in **Settings → Workout Screen**.

- **Suggested weight.** Under an exercise that has a rep range (like 8-12) and a last time, a small card shows what you did and what to try today, with a **Fill in** button (and an Undo). The rule is the usual one: hit the top of the range on every working set and it suggests a heavier weight; miss the bottom of the range on a set and it keeps the weight; otherwise it asks for one more rep. "Heavier" means the next weight you can really load. A barbell uses the plates from your gym profile, and dumbbells use that gym's dumbbell range, so at the heaviest dumbbell it says to add reps instead. It stays quiet when there is nothing to compare (first time, AMRAP, pyramids, timed sets). **Pre-fill (2.79.0):** the first time you open a day each week, every set is filled in with that suggestion, and a **Fill in suggested weights** button at the top of the workout does the whole day in one tap (with Undo). Settings → Workout screen → **Pre-fill the whole workout** turns the automatic part off.
- **Machine taken? (2.81.0).** Someone is on the lat pulldown? Tap the small **🔁 Taken?** next to the rest time at the top of the exercise (it shows on machines, cables, barbells and the Smith machine). It swaps in the closest match from Similar exercises, one that is not already in the day and, when there is one, on different equipment, in the same spot with the same sets and reps. The card is refilled from the new exercise's own history, and the toast has **Undo**. **🔁** next to it tries the next closest, and **↩ (the original)** goes back. It is a loan for one day: the next day you open the app the original is back by itself, unless you already ticked a set on the swap. The normal swap button stays permanent. Settings → Workout screen → **Machine taken? button** turns it off.
- **Fill in the next set.** Check off a set and the next empty set gets the same weight and reps. Warm-up sets are skipped.
- **One-tap log (new in 2.91.0).** Tap ✓ on a set whose boxes are still empty and it is logged with the grey numbers in the boxes, which are what you did on that set last time (a set past last time's count uses last time's last set). A box you already typed in keeps your number. Warm-up sets only copy a warm-up from last time, and cardio rows are left alone. With no last time, ✓ works as before. **One-tap log** turns it off.
- **Quick +/− buttons (new in 2.91.0).** While you type in a weight or reps box, a slim row under that set has **−5 / +5** (your weight step from Settings) and **−1 / +1** reps. They start from the number in the box, or the grey one, and the keyboard stays open. **Quick +/− buttons** turns them off.
- **Timer starts on your first set.** The workout timer shows **Ready** until you check off a set, so opening a day and getting a coffee doesn't count. Tap the pill to start it early.
- **Look over before saving.** **Finish Workout** shows a short summary first: time, sets, volume compared with the last time you did that day, any new records, and a note if some sets aren't checked off. **Keep going** or **Save workout**. This screen is also the "are you sure?" step: Finish Workout never saves straight away, and **Save workout** ignores taps for the first moment so an accidental double-tap can't save it. Turn the look-over off and Finish Workout asks a plain **Finish workout?** question instead.
- **Favorites and Recent.** In the exercise picker, tap the ☆ next to any exercise to pin it. **★ Favorites** and **Recent** sit at the top of the picker, and the ☆ is also on the exercise's page.
- **Set row options.** Choose what shows on every set: **Last time**, the **warm-up / drop tag**, and **Est. 1RM**. Weight, reps and the check button always show.
- **Left-hand mode** moves the check button and the **+ Set / − Set** buttons to the left side.

### Gyms and equipment (new in 2.8.0)

**Settings → Gyms & Equipment.** Add a profile for each place you train: your home garage, a commercial gym, a hotel gym or bodyweight only. Each one has its own **bar weight, plates and dumbbell range** (smallest, heaviest and step), and a list of **equipment it has** (barbell, EZ-bar, dumbbells, machines, cables, Smith machine, kettlebells, bands, bodyweight and, since 2.97.0, **bench, pull-up bar, suspension trainer (TRX), stability ball, medicine ball, foam roller and sled**). An exercise that needs a bench (bench, incline and decline work, unless it is a machine) or a pull-up bar (pull-ups, chin-ups, hanging work) is left out when the gym has none, even if it has the dumbbells. openGym exercises use openGym's own equipment field rather than a guess from the name.

- If a workout has an exercise the active gym can't do, its card says so with **Find a swap**.
- Once you have two gyms, a small **📍 gym chip** at the top of the workout switches between them.
- The plate calculator and the suggested weights follow the active gym. Until you edit anything there is one implicit gym ("My gym") and nothing is hidden.

### Build me a workout (new in 2.19.0, much bigger in 2.21.0)

Home → **Build a Workout** (in the **Start training** row), or **Build a workout** in the side menu. Pick what to train, how long you have and a goal, and it builds a session for you. No account and no internet needed.

- **A different mix every time (2.21.0).** It used to hand out much the same lifts each time. Now every build is a weighted draw from the best choices for each muscle, and it steers away from what it gave you in the last few builds, from exercises you swapped away, and from lifts in your last three saved workouts. Build Push eight times and you get eight different sessions, with no big lift missing and no odd picks.
- **Variety:** **Favourites** leans on lifts you already do and the classics. **Balanced** is a bit of both. **Mix it up** (the default) goes for fresh picks. Hit **Shuffle** for another version.
- **Lock, swap, remove, add (2.21.0).** After it builds, each exercise has a **🔒** (keep it when you Shuffle: **Shuffle the rest**), a **↻** (swap just that one for another exercise for the same muscle) and a **✕** (remove it). **＋ Add an exercise** adds one more for the muscle that has the fewest. The time updates as you change it.
- **What to train:** **Pick for me**, Push, Pull, Legs, Upper body, Lower body, Full body, Chest & back, Shoulders & arms, Arms, Glutes or Core, or tap any muscles yourself (13 to choose from). **Pick for me** looks at your recovery map and this week's sets and chooses the freshest group (Push, Pull or Legs), leaves out a muscle that is still recovering, and tells you why. With no history yet it starts you on Full body.
- **How long:** 15, 20, 30, 45, 60, 75 or 90 minutes. It counts sets and rest times and lands within about 10% of the time you asked for. Short on time, it leaves out the smallest muscles and says so.
- **Goal:** **Build muscle** (big lifts 6-10 reps, isolation 10-15), **Get stronger** (big lifts 3-6 reps with longer rests) or **Tone & endurance** (12-15 and 15-20 reps with shorter rests).
- **More options (2.21.0):** **Equipment**: your gym (default), Dumbbells, Machines & cables, Barbell or Bodyweight, always inside what your active gym has. **Number of exercises**: Auto, or 3, 4, 5, 6 or 8 (if that many will not fit the time, it says how long they take). **Style**: Straight sets, or **Supersets (saves time)**, which pairs opposite muscles (chest with back, biceps with triceps) and fits more into the same time. **Extras**: a **Core finisher** adds one abs exercise at the end.
- **What it picks from:** the exercise library, using each exercise's muscle. It only offers what the **active gym** can do, starts each muscle with a big lift, never picks the same movement twice if there is another choice, favours well-known lifts, and leaves out jumps, carries, stretches and cardio. The first lift of a big muscle gets 4 sets (3 when time is tight).
- **Start workout** opens it as a normal **Freestyle** workout (supersets stay linked), so you can still swap, add, delete, reorder, **Share day** or save it as a routine. If your last Freestyle workout still has exercises it asks before replacing them. The app remembers your last choices. The **Build** and **Shuffle** buttons stay in view at the bottom while you scroll the list.

### Work check-in (new in 2.19.0)

Landscaping, building, moving house: a hard day at work uses the same back, grip and legs you lift with. Home has a one-row **Work today** card with three buttons: **Light or off**, **Normal** and **Heavy**. Tap one at the end of the day (tap it again to clear it, or tap **Yesterday?** to fill in yesterday). It is saved per day and is part of your backup.

- **Only Heavy changes anything.** A heavy day counts in the **Recovery map** as extra hard sets for lower back, forearms and grip, legs, shoulders, traps and back, so the Muscles tab, the "still recovering" line on Up next and **Pick for me** all know your back is tired. Chest and arms are not affected. Light and Normal days are just a record.
- **Suggested weight stays put.** On a heavy day, lifts for those muscles keep their weight instead of suggesting more ("Heavy work day, so keep this weight and add a rep or two"). Chest and arm lifts still go up.
- **Build me a workout goes easy.** It leaves out deadlifts, bent-over rows, shrugs and carries, prefers machines and cables, and builds one exercise fewer.
- **A heavy day reshuffles the week (new in 2.44.0).** If you mark Heavy and the workout up next is hard on those same muscles (Legs, or a back day), the Work card offers a lighter day from the same program to do today instead: *"Heavy work today, and Legs is hard on the same muscles. Chest & Arms is easier on them."* **Do Chest & Arms today** moves it up; **Keep Legs** hides the offer for today. How hard a day is on you comes from the same weights as the recovery map (lower back and forearms 4, legs 3, shoulders, traps and back 2, everything else 0), averaged over the day's hard sets. A day is only offered a swap at 1.5 or more, and the day offered has to score at least 1 lower. Not offered for Freestyle, after you have already trained today, in the "Yesterday?" view, or when **Go easier after heavy work days** is off.
- **Switch:** **Settings → Workout Screen → Go easier after heavy work days** (on by default). Turn it off and the check-in only feeds the recovery map. Hide the card with **Settings → Home screen**.

### Guided mode (sets added in 2.36.0, each-side note in 2.38.0, supersets in 2.51.0)

Tap **Guided mode** on the workout screen for one exercise at a time with big steppers: weight and reps, one big **Log set** button, and **Prev**, **Swap** and **Skip** at the bottom. Chips show every set (✓ with what you lifted, the current one highlighted). It writes into the normal workout screen, so nothing is lost if you switch back. Timed and other special exercises open in the normal view.

A set removed from the middle moves later logged sets up, so nothing is lost.
- **Extra sets** are the ones you add on top of the program's own. They show with a dashed chip and a **+** (like **S4+**), and the line under the name says "extra set".
- **Skipping an extra set deletes it.** On an extra set the bottom button says **✕ Skip set** and just removes that set, then moves on when the exercise has nothing left to log. On one of the program's own sets, **Skip ▶** skips the exercise as before, and any unlogged extra sets on it go too, so they do not sit there unchecked when you finish.
- **All done screen:** after the last set you can finish the workout or add **one more set** of the last exercise.
- Your **machine setup note** for the exercise shows under its name (see below).
- **Supersets, tri-sets, giant sets and circuits (new in 2.51.0).** Exercises linked with **Superset with next** (the A1 / A2 / A3 groups on the workout screen) now come up as **one screen**. One big **✔ Log round 1** button logs set 1 of every exercise in order, through the same buttons as the normal screen, so the rest timer, PRs and history work the same. The rest timer comes up after the last exercise of the round, and the Log button moves up above it so the timer never covers it. Then it moves on to round 2, starting from the weight and reps you just used.
  - The chips show **R1, R2, R3** (a round is done when every exercise has that set). **＋ Round** and **− Round** add or remove one set on every exercise in the group. An extra round shows with a dashed **R6+** chip, and **✕ Skip round** deletes it. **Prev** and **Skip** move a whole group at a time, and the top line says "Exercises 3–4 of 16".
  - If the exercises in a group have different numbers of sets, the later rounds only show the exercises that still have a set; the others say "all 3 sets done". A timed exercise in a group is listed with "timed or tracked differently" and is logged in the normal view, while the rest of the group still logs here.
  - Exercises that are not linked keep the same single screen as before.
  - Guided mode is now readable in the light look too (the title, numbers and buttons were white on white).

- Starts by itself when you check off a set (you can turn this off in Settings).

### Workout timer (top of the screen)

A timer starts when you open a workout. **Tap it** for the controls:

| Option | What it does |
|---|---|
| **Pause / Resume** | For a long break or a phone call. Paused time is not counted. |

### Tabata timer

An interval timer for HIIT. Open it from the side menu (**Tabata Timer**) or from a Tabata exercise in a program (**Launch Tabata Timer**).

- A ring drains over each part: **Get ready** (amber), **Work** (your accent colour) and **Rest** (blue). Round bars show how many rounds are done, and it tells you what is **next** and how much time is **left**.
- **Pause / Resume**, **start over** (left button) and **skip** to the next part (right button). When all rounds are done, **Go again** runs it once more.
- The sliders button at the top changes the timing: **work, rest, rounds and get-ready time**, or a preset (Classic 20/10 ×8, 30/15, 40/20, 45/15, 30/30). Rest can be 0.
- Opened from the side menu, it remembers your last timing. A program's own Tabata exercise uses the program's numbers, and changes there apply to that run only.
- It beeps at 3, 2, 1 and at each change (following your Sound and Vibrate settings) and keeps the screen on, on phones that allow it. It times itself from the clock, so if your phone sleeps for a bit, it catches up to the right place when you return.
- The layout leaves room for the notch and home bar. That spacing is done the standard way but has not been tried on a real iPhone.

### 1-rep-max (1RM) calculator

Home → **1RM Calculator**. Type a hard set (for example 225 × 5) and it estimates your one-rep max (262.5). Below is a chart from **100% down to 50%**: the weight at each percent and about how many reps that is.

### Strength

- **PR attempt planner (new in 2.36.0):** pick a lift (your best estimated 1RM is shown), a **target for one rep** (it starts a little above your best, in steps of your plate increment, with a plain note on how realistic it is: under 3% above your best is very doable, up to 6% is a real stretch, more is ambitious) and a **test day** (1 to 4 weeks out, or any date). It builds:
  - **The week before:** a **last heavy day** (3 sets of 3 at about 85%, 6 days out), a **light day** (3 sets of 2 or 3 at about 65%, 3 days out) and a **rest day** the day before, with a button for easy mobility. If the date is closer, the heavy day moves in or drops out.
  - **Attempt day:** a warm-up ladder you can tick off (empty bar, then about 40, 55, 70, 80 and 90% of the target), then **Attempt 1 · opener** (about 93%), **Attempt 2** (97%) and **Attempt 3 · goal** (the target). Every step is a weight your gym can actually load, with the **plates per side** and a rest time. For lifts that are not barbell lifts the steps round to your increment. A note says to take attempt 2 only if the opener moved fast and attempt 3 only if attempt 2 was smooth.

### Plateau tips

A lift counts as **stalled** when you've done it **3 or more times in the last few weeks** and none of those sessions beat the best you had before. You pick how many weeks with the **2 wk / 3 wk / 4 wk / 6 wk / 8 wk** buttons on the Plateau check card (Progress → Overview); **4 weeks** to start, and your choice is remembered. Each stuck lift shows how many weeks it has been since your best, for example **6 weeks**. A lift you've stopped doing drops off by itself, and matching your best counts as no improvement. The app then works out why and suggests something that fits:

| What happened | Tip |
|---|---|
| Same weight every session | Add a rep to every set before adding weight, or take a smaller jump (2.5 lb / 1 kg). |
| Reps climbing at the same weight | Keep going. Add weight when you hit the top of your rep range. |
| Added weight but lost reps | Stay at that weight until the reps come back. |
| Down 5% or more from your best | Check sleep and food, and consider a lighter week. |
| Anything else | Try a small weight drop, a different rep range, or a similar exercise for a few weeks. |

The tip shows on the Overview tab, on the exercise card in your workout, and on the exercise's chart screen.

### Group board (rebuilt in 2.53.0, called the family board until 2.90.0)

**Progress → Social.** There is no server of ours, so the board runs either on codes that travel in any chat, or, since 2.89.0, live through a free public relay. The old board only changed when somebody sent a fresh code, and people who had not sent one that week fell off it. Now:

- **Live family board (new in 2.89.0).** Progress → Social → **Family board** now starts with a **Live family board** card. One person taps **Start a board** and sends the **family code** (16 letters and numbers like `K7QM-2XPD-9TRA-HW4C`, with a **Send to the family** button). Everyone else taps **Join with a code** and types it once (small letters, spaces or a whole pasted message are fine). After that everybody's update arrives on its own: when you open the app, when you come back to it, about every 40 seconds while the board is on screen and every 4 minutes otherwise, and your own update goes out after a workout or when something changed (at most once a minute, and again about every 6 hours). The card shows **Live · 2 min ago**, **Can't connect · retrying** or **Offline**, with **Refresh**, **Family code** (shows it again) and **Leave** (forgets the code; the people already on your board stay).
  - **It is off until somebody starts or joins a board.** Until then the app makes no network request for it at all. Offline it does nothing and the old buttons below it (**Send my update**, **Paste update**) keep working; they are now called **Or send by message** when the live board is on, for someone who is not on it.
  - **No accounts and no server of ours.** Phones swap small messages through the free public relay **ntfy.sh**. The family code is never sent: two things are worked out from it on the phone (PBKDF2, 100,000 rounds), a long random-looking mailbox name and an AES-256-GCM key, and every message is encrypted with that key before it leaves the phone. The relay only sees scrambled text, the mailbox name, message sizes, timing and phone network addresses, not names or numbers. A message that does not decrypt (another family, rubbish) is ignored. **Anyone who has the code can read and post updates, so share it only with your family.** A code has 80 bits, which cannot be guessed.
  - **What is sent** is the same update code the board always used (name, level, streak, last 4 weeks, lift bests), so the rankings, **Boss fight** and **Family recap** follow it without changes. Challenges and Buddies workouts still travel by message.
  - **How long updates last (2.89.1).** The relay itself only keeps messages about 12 hours, and that is the relay's own setting, which the app cannot change. So the phones carry the memory: each phone keeps what it has seen, and remembers when it last saw each person in the mailbox. When somebody it knows has had nothing in the mailbox for 6 hours, it puts the latest update it has of them back (scrambled, with their original time stamp, so it never looks newer than the real one; people not seen for 45 days are left out). Whoever opens the app tops it up, so a person's update outlives the 12 hours as long as somebody opens the app now and then, and a new member sees everybody straight away. When a phone sees a name it has never seen before, it also sends its own update again so the newcomer gets it. People who have not opened the app lately show as "updated 3 days ago". ntfy.sh is a free community service with no promise it stays up; if it is down or blocked the card says so and everything else carries on. Live sync needs a current Chrome (it uses the browser's built-in encryption). It could only be tested here against a fake relay that follows ntfy's documented behaviour, so it is the first thing to try on the real phones.

- **One update code carries everything.** Your last four weeks, streak, progress and strength rating are in a single update. **Send my update** shares it in one tap, and **Paste update** reads the one in your clipboard. You can also paste a whole message into the box and tap **Add**.
- **Every workout you send also updates the board.** Sending a finished workout, a card or a challenge to buddies adds your update to the same message, so the messages you already send keep the board current without extra steps.
- **Nobody disappears.** People who have not sent anything this week stay on the board, with how long ago they last updated ("updated 4 days ago"). A stale row has an **Ask for update** button that sends your own update along with a request for theirs.
- **Fair between different people.** Volume and weights are gone, because they depend on how big and strong someone is. The contests are:
  - **Score:** 100 points for hitting **your own** weekly goal, plus 10 for each new record (up to 5). Goals count between 3 and 6 days, so setting a goal of 1 cannot be farmed.
  - **Consistency:** days trained against your own goal.
  - **Streak:** weeks in a row you hit your goal.
  - **Records:** new personal records.
  - **Progress:** how much your main lifts went up over 4 weeks, in percent. A beginner adding 10 lb and an advanced lifter adding 20 lb are compared by percent, not pounds.
  - **Strength:** your StrengthLevel rating out of 100 for your body weight and sex (the average level of your 5 best exercises).
- **Weekly crowns and a season.** The top score of each week earns a 👑, and a table shows crowns and points over the last 8 weeks that had two or more people. **This week / Last week** switches the view.
- **Boss fight (new in 2.82.0):** Progress → Social → Family → **Boss fight**. Every week a new boss shows up (12 rotate, the same for everybody with nothing to send) and the family beats it together. It is worked out from the update codes that already make the board, so **every update anyone sends also hits the boss**. Each day you train hits for **100** (one day over your own weekly goal counts too) and each new record is a **50 critical hit** (up to 3). The boss has **100 HP for every day of everyone's weekly goal**, so it is fair when people train different amounts, and one person doing a day extra can carry someone who had a bad week. Someone who has not sent an update yet still counts in the health and shows as **waiting**, so the bar drops as updates arrive. **Rally the family** shares the boss's health with your update. Every boss you beat in a row makes the next one **10% tougher** (up to +50%); a missed week resets it. A win you landed a hit on is kept, pays **40 coins plus 10 for each other person who hit it** (up to 100), shows in How coins work, and unlocks the titles **Boss Slayer** (1 win), **Boss Hunter** (5) and **Boss Legend** (15). A toast and confetti mark the win. Past bosses show as small ✓ / ✗ tiles.
- **Family recap (new in 2.63.0):** a **Family recap** card sits above the board. It shows who won last week (or this week), everyone's days against their own goal, records and points, a few highlights (most records, longest streak, best progress over 4 weeks, the workouts you did together) and who has not sent an update yet, with the board's **Ask** button. **Share as picture** makes a 1080 × 1350 image for the family chat; **Copy as text** copies the same recap as plain lines. It uses the updates the board already has, so nobody has to send anything new. On Monday and Tuesday Home shows one line when last week's recap is ready (**Open** or **Later**, once per week).
- **Family challenge:** pick a contest for the week (highest score, best consistency or most records), tap **Start a challenge** and **Share challenge + my update**. Challenges started before 2.53.0 (most workouts, volume, sets) still finish and show.
- **Everyone needs 2.53.0 to read the new update.** Old codes (weekly codes, buddy workouts, reactions, wall cards) still work in both directions, but the new update code only shows on phones that have updated. Open the app online once, then close and reopen it.
- Codes only hold names, week totals and ratings. Nothing is stored online unless you start or join a live board; then the relay holds your scrambled updates for about 12 hours.

### Coach mode (new in 2.47.0)

For running a practice from one phone: a team, a class, a group of friends. Open it from **Cardio & Sports → 🧑‍🏫 Coach mode** (the green card under the Start timer buttons). Everything stays on the phone and is in your backup. It is separate from your own lifting, like the rest of Cardio & Sports.

Tap a drill for the team board and the team average by day.
- **Group timer.** One interval clock for the whole group. Set **work** seconds, **rotate** seconds and **rounds**, or start from a preset (**Tabata 20/10 ×8**, **Circuit 40/20 ×6**, **Sprints 30/30 ×10**, **Stations 60/15 ×6**). ## Good to know

**Works offline.** After the first load, everything (programs, exercises, charts, styling, fonts) is stored in the app. Only the YouTube "How to do it" links, getting updates and voice logging on most phones need internet. The family board works offline too: it is made of codes you send each other, with no server of ours behind it, and the optional live board simply waits until you are online again. Close the app and reopen it in airplane mode, or refresh the page offline, and it opens with every feature.

**Offline, in detail (new in 2.58.0).**
- **A weak connection opens the saved app.** A phone with one bar of signal, a gym wifi that has not logged you in, or a dead mobile connection can leave a request hanging for a minute without ever failing, which is not the same as airplane mode. The app used to wait on it. Now, if the network has not answered within 4 seconds, it opens the saved copy and keeps trying in the background to refresh it.
- **A wifi login page can no longer replace the app.** Hotel, gym and airport wifi often answer every address with a login page. The app now checks that what came back is really its own file, and otherwise acts as if it were offline.
- **It tells you.** Settings → App says **Works offline ✓**, the Settings list shows "Version 2.98.0 · works offline ✓", and the side menu has a small "offline ✓" next to the version. When the saved copy is missing (the phone cleaned it up, or the first save never finished) it says **Not saved for offline yet** with a **Save now** button, and it saves the copy again by itself the next time you open the app or come back online.
- **Home screen app:** it asks the phone to keep the saved copy and your data from being cleaned up automatically. Phones allow this for installed apps without asking.
- Saving a new version is all or nothing: the app is only replaced once all three files (`index.html`, `exercises.js`, `programs.js`) came back whole (the openGym library, `opengym-exercises.js`, is saved too but never holds an update back), so you never end up with half of an update.

**iPhone**
- Updates can lag. Close the app fully and reopen it online.
- If you don't use the app for a long time, iPhone may clear its stored files. Keep a backup.
- It has not been tested on a real iPhone.

**The phone closes the app (new in 2.50.1).** Phones close apps they have not used for a while, or when the screen is off and memory is short. Your sets and the workout timer were always saved, but the app used to start again on Home. Now it remembers the screen you were on and how far down you had scrolled, and opens straight back on it. In a workout it keeps your place for 12 hours (the same as the workout timer); on any other page, 3 hours, and after that it opens on Home as usual. If you had just finished a workout it opens on Home, and it only returns to a workout if it is still the same program and day. Turn it off in **Settings → Reopen where I left off**.

**About the numbers.** Recovery times and target ranges are estimates and typical guidelines, not medical or coaching advice. Form tips are general guidance: if something hurts, stop and ask a coach or doctor.

**What a web app can't do.** Apple Health or Strava sync, a watch app, home-screen widgets, a lock-screen timer, or reminder notifications. Those need a native app or a server.

**About Kris Gethin 12 Weeks to Muscle.** The sets, reps and rest were copied from the plan day by day, with a few gaps filled in: where no rest time was given, 90 seconds is used (60 seconds for 15+ reps); "Seated Calf Raise" on day 19 has no set count, so it uses 3; day 58 is missing the name of one exercise, so it shows as **Exercise of Your Choice**; and a few exercises where the sets and the listed rep targets disagree. Each of these is noted on the exercise, including which number was used. Exercise names were matched to the PulseTrack list, and 26 new exercises were added for moves that did not exist yet.

**About the 12-week programs.** The source plans had a few gaps, handled like this:
- Ferrigno: the heading says weeks 5–8, but the schedule says weeks 7–12.
- Arnold: no phase lengths were given, so each phase is 3 weeks.
- Mountain Dog: one week repeats for 12 weeks.
- Phil Heath Phase 3: cardio has no time listed.
- Ronnie Coleman: crunches have no sets or reps listed.
- Ronnie Coleman 6-On, 1-Off Split: the video report names the muscle groups only, so the exercises, sets and reps come from other write-ups of his training (Generation Iron, Built for Athletes, Fitness Volt, Iron Man magazine, Hunt Fitness). Only the 20 steps on the walking lunges (the sources say about 30 meters a set) and the 12 weeks are ours. Calves sit on his leg day here; in the write-ups they were on Saturday.
- Ronnie Coleman Olympia Powerbuilding: Exercise.com presents it as his regimen and lists it as 4 weeks, but its page shows week 1 only, so that week repeats. Exercise.com gives every exercise 1:00 rest; the sets of 2 on the deadlift and squat get 3 minutes here.
- Ronnie Coleman High-Rep 6-Day Split: it is the routine Jacked Gorilla and DMoose publish; neither names an original source and DMoose calls it 'not an exact replica'. Where a source says '3-4 sets' the lower number is used; rest times and the 12 weeks are ours.
- Ronnie Coleman-Inspired 4-Day Upper/Lower: Set For Set wrote it, not Coleman. The site gives four workouts without weekdays or rest times; both are ours, and so are the 12 weeks.

### Building and testing (new in 2.68.0)

`index.html` used to be made by running about ninety patch scripts one after another, and those scripts were lost. It is now made from plain source files, so a change is an edit to one file:

- **`src/index.template.html`** is the page. Wherever it says `{{file:js/...}}` or `{{file:css/...}}` inside a `<script>` or `<style>` tag, the build puts that file's text there. The order of the lines is the order the scripts run. `{{VERSION}}` is replaced by `src/VERSION`.
- **`src/js/NNN-*.js`** are the scripts, in order. `004-app-core.js` is the app itself; each `v2.xx.x` file is one feature, added on top. The `999-` file always stays last.
- **Build:** `python3 tools/build.py` writes `index.html`; `python3 tools/build.py --check` says whether `index.html` matches `src/` (the first test run checks it too). Never edit `index.html` by hand.
- **New feature:** `python3 tools/new_module.py 2.71.0 "what it does"` makes the next `src/js` file and adds it to the page before the last script. For a change to something that already exists, edit its file.
- **New release:** `python3 tools/bump.py 2.71.0` sets `src/VERSION`, adds one to the cache number in `sw.js` (so phones fetch the new files), changes the version lines at the top of this file, and rebuilds. Then add the version history entry here.
- **Tests:** `python3 tests/run_all.py` runs them all (`smoke.py`, `calendar.py`, `programs.py`, `looks.py`, `themes.py`, `home.py`, `weeks.py`, `rewards.py`, `fill.py`, `backup.py`, `taken.py`, `boss.py`, `shield.py`, `ui.py`, `install.py`, `live.py`, `group.py`, `battle.py`, `fast.py`, `wrapped.py`, `nofront.py`, `isodip.py`, `split64.py`, `daysdone.py`, `nodips.py`, `opengym.py`, `equipment.py`, `importapps.py`) in a real Chromium at phone size; each file also runs on its own. They need Python with Playwright installed.
- **The Neater look's outlines:** `src/css/11-pt-neat.css` is written by hand. `src/css/10-pt-neat-borders.css` is generated: after adding a feature that draws its own bordered cards, run `python3 tools/neat_borders.py` and it writes the matching quiet-outline rules. Dark themes give cards the lighter shade in `applyTheme` (`--z900`), which is what lifts them without an outline.
- **Programs and exercises** are still edited in `programs.js` and `exercises.js` (see below). The build does not touch them.

### How program changes reach phones

- A program that is **new** in `programs.js` shows up on phones automatically.
- A program that **already existed** and was edited is *not* replaced on phones, so your progress is safe. To get the changed version, delete the program in the app and use **Add Back a Built-in Program**.
- Two exceptions. **Week info:** if a built-in program is still the same as when you got it but did not yet have week numbers (FST-7 Big and Ripped, Jim Stoppani Shortcut to Shred) or a week plan (Kai Greene, FST-7 Level 1), the app fills that in by itself the next time it opens. Only the missing week numbers are added; exercises, edits and history are never touched, and a program you rebuilt yourself is left alone. A "Week 3" you had typed in before becomes week 3 of the program, once. **Longer programs (2.48.0):** a built-in program that used to be one week on repeat gets its extra weeks added to your copy, as new days after the ones you have, when your copy still has its original days numbered 1 to n. Your own edits to those days stay, and nothing is replaced. If you removed or reordered days in it, it is left as it was. Rest days that an old program had stored as an exercise (USN 5 Day Mass Plan) become real rest days. **Wording:** if a built-in program still carries an old source note, the app swaps in the cleaned text by itself. It only touches text containing an old note, so anything you wrote or edited is never changed, and no exercise, set, rep or logged workout changes.

### Adding programs and exercises

- **Easiest:** build a program inside the app, or import a `.json` file with the Import button.
- **Permanent for everyone:** add it to `programs.js`, one object per program with `id`, `title`, `description`, `tags` and `days`. Each day has `dayNumber`, `title`, `estimatedMinutes` and `exercises` (each with `id`, `name`, `sets`, `reps`, `restSeconds`, `type`, `notes`). For two-a-day programs, title the sessions like `"Mon AM: Chest"` and `"Mon PM: Arms"` (or `"Chest (AM) + Arms (PM)"`) and the app adds the sun and moon look, the AM+PM tag and the Two-a-day shelf by itself; add `"Two-A-Days"` to `tags` too. For the Programs screen to show the athlete, goal and level, add `pro` (a name, or "" for none), `goal` (`"mass"`, `"cutting"`, `"strength"` or `"balanced"`), `level` (`"beginner"`, `"intermediate"` or `"advanced"`) and `perWeek` (workouts a week, or `null`). For a program that lists every week, add `week: 1…12` to every day. For a program whose few days repeat over several weeks, add `weekPlan: [{ weeks: [1, 2, 3], days: [1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6] }, { weeks: [4, 5, 6], days: [7, 8, 9, 10, 11, 12] }]` to the program itself. Both give it the pinned **Switch week** bar. An empty `exercises` list is a rest day.
- **New exercises:** use **My Exercises** in the app, or add them to `exercises.js` for everyone. One line each: `{ name: "Zercher Deadlift", category: "Back", muscle: "Lower Back" }`. `muscle` is what Similar exercises matches on (the list of muscles is at the top of `exercises.js`). Without it the app works the muscle out from the name, and only falls back to the category when it can't. If the exercise is also known by other names, list them in `aka`: `{ name: "Rope Pushdown", category: "Triceps", aka: ["Triceps Rope Pushdown"] }`. Those names are searchable and count as the same exercise (same muscle group, never suggested as a swap for itself). Only give it its own entry if it really is a different exercise (a straight-bar pushdown is not a rope pushdown). To give an exercise its own form tips, add `tips` (3 or 4 short steps) and `avoid` (the usual mistake): `{ name: "Zercher Deadlift", category: "Back", tips: ["Hold the bar in the crooks of your elbows", "Keep your chest up and your back flat", "Stand up by driving your hips forward"], avoid: "Rounding your back at the start." }`.

Keep every `id` unique. New programs reach phones on the next update.
