# PulseTrack 💪

**A free workout tracker that lives on your phone and works with no internet.**
Pick a program, log your sets, beat your records. No account, no ads, no sign-up. Your data stays on your phone.

*Version 2.49.0*

PulseTrack installs to your home screen like a normal app. It comes with **56 ready-made programs** (nearly all of them planned over several weeks, six of them two-a-day, with morning and evening sessions; 12-week plans from Arnold, Ronnie Coleman, Phil Heath and Kris Gethin, plus routines of pro bodybuilders like Mike Mentzer, Dorian Yates, Frank Zane, Chris Bumstead, Flex Wheeler, Lee Priest, Layne Norton, Reg Park, Dave Draper and Dexter Jackson) and **624 exercises**, and you can add your own of both. It has rest and interval timers, a separate **cardio and sports logger**, charts, personal records, a 1-rep-max calculator, body tracking, and a muscle map that shows what is recovered and what is behind. You can change how it looks (light or dark, 13 themes, any colour, 8 fonts).

## Install and update

**Install.** Open the app link in your phone's browser once, with internet.

| Phone | Steps |
|---|---|
| iPhone (Safari) | Share button → **Add to Home Screen** → Add |
| Android (Chrome) | ⋮ menu → **Install app** (or **Add to Home screen**) |

After that it opens full screen like any app and works offline.

**Update.** When new files have been uploaded to GitHub:

1. Open the app **with internet**.
2. Close it fully and open it again.
3. You have the new version, and your data is still there.

Android updates on the first refresh. iPhone can be slower: if you don't see the update, close the app completely and reopen it while online.

## Your first workout

1. Open the app. You land on **Home**. Nothing is picked for you.
2. Tap **Choose a Program** and look around. Opening a program only lets you look, it never makes it yours. Tap **Select** on the one you want (more in [Your current program](#your-current-program)), or start **Freestyle** for a free workout.
3. Tap a day to see every exercise with its sets, reps and rest.
4. For each set, type your **weight** and **reps**, then tap the check mark. The rest timer starts by itself.
5. Tap **Finish Workout**. You get a summary, and the next day lines up as **Up next**.

### The Programs screen (new look in 2.37.0, two-a-day shelf in 2.40.0)

The list used to be a tall stack of buttons and big cards. It is now built to be scanned:

- **Current program card** on top: the program you are on, your week, and the next workout (**Next: Week 3 · Day 2: Pull**), with an **Open** button. If nothing is selected it says so.
- **Two-a-day shelf (new in 2.40.0):** right under the current program, a row of sunrise-to-night cards, one per two-a-day program. Each shows the athlete, the title, the **AM** session and the **PM** session of its first double day (for example **AM Chest & Back / PM Thighs, Calves & Abs**), and days a week. Swipe sideways for the rest, tap a card to open the program, or tap **See all**. The shelf only shows on the plain All view; search or any filter hides it so the list stays the list.
- **Tools in one compact grid:** **Build me a program** across the top, then **Create your own**, **Paste a program**, **Import a file**, **Share link**, **Guided mobility** and **Stretch routine**, and (new in 2.43.0) **Steal a day** and **Playlist** on a row of their own. Same buttons as before, a third of the height.
- **Search** by program name, athlete, tag, workout name or **exercise**. Typing "hack squat" lists every program that has one; "zane" lists his three; "mentzer pullover" narrows to the one program that has both. Every word has to match, and the match is highlighted.
- **Filter chips with counts:** **All**, **Pro plans** (tied to a named athlete), **Two-a-day** (any program with morning and evening sessions), **Mass**, **Cutting**, **Strength**, **Beginner** and **Mine** (programs you made or imported).
- **One slim row per program:** a coloured badge with the athlete's initials (the same colour for every program of one athlete), the title, and one line of facts, like **Mass · 6 days/wk · 12 weeks · Advanced**. Two-a-day programs also carry a small gold **☀ ☾ AM+PM** tag (**☀ wk 10–12** when only some weeks train twice a day). Rows are grouped as **Your programs**, **Pro programs** (by athlete) and **More programs**. The current program's row says **✓ Current**.
- **Tap a row to open it. Tap ⋯ for the rest:** **Open program**, **Steal a day from it** (2.43.0), **Make it my current program**, **Share** and **Delete from this phone** (still asks first; a built-in program can be added back later).
- The labels (athlete, goal, level, days a week) come from `programs.js`. Programs you made yourself get a guess from their title and tags.

#### How a two-a-day program looks inside (new in 2.40.0)

Open one and the day list shows what each session is. A day titled **Mon AM: Chest & Back** reads **☀ MONDAY · MORNING** over **Chest & Back**, with a gold bar down the left; **Mon PM: Thighs, Calves & Abs** reads **☾ MONDAY · EVENING** with an indigo bar and sits tight under it, so each day's pair is one block. A note above the list says how it runs: do the morning session, eat and rest, then the evening session a few hours later. Each session is still its own workout, with its own sets, timer and finish screen, and it counts when you finish it, so **Up next** goes from Monday morning to Monday evening to Tuesday. The progress line says **sessions done** instead of days. Programs that put both in one day (Franco Columbu's **Chest, Shoulders, Abs (AM) + Arms (PM)**) show the two sessions on two lines inside the card.

It works for your own programs too: name the days **Mon AM: Legs** and **Mon PM: Arms** (or **Legs (AM) + Arms (PM)**) and they get the same look, the AM+PM tag, and a place on the Two-a-day shelf.

### Steal a day (new in 2.43.0)

Take one workout from any program without taking the whole program. **Programs → Steal a day** opens a finder:

- **Pick a muscle** (Chest, Back, Shoulders, Arms, Quads, Hams & glutes, Calves, Abs) and it lists the best days for it from every program in the library plus your own, best first. A day is ranked by how many of its hard sets hit that muscle, and only days that are mostly that muscle (at least 40% of the sets and at least 5 sets) are listed. Each row shows the day, the athlete and program, its biggest muscle groups with set counts, and the number of exercises and minutes.
- **Or search** an athlete, a program or a day name ("Cutler", "back width", "PHAT"). The list shows 30 at a time with **Show more**.
- **Tap a day** to see all its exercises with sets and reps, then choose what to do with it:
  - **Do it now:** a one-off **Freestyle** workout with those exercises. Your program is not touched, and your current program stays your current program. If your Freestyle workout already has sets logged, it asks before replacing it.
  - **Add to my current program:** a new day at the end of it. Only offered when your current program is one you made yourself (see below).
  - **Add to one of my programs:** pick any program you made, and the day goes at the end of it.
  - **Start a new program from it:** a program of your own with just that day. Add more days to it later from the same screen.
- **Nothing in a built-in program is ever edited.** The day is copied with fresh ids, and supersets are kept (the last exercise is never left linked to nothing). That is why **Add to my current program** is not offered while a built-in program is current: use **Add to one of my programs** or **Start a new program from it** instead.
- **Other ways in:** a program's **⋯** menu (**Steal a day from it**, showing only that program's days), the workout screen's **⋯ More → Steal this day…** (for the day on screen), and the **Weak points** card on Progress → Strength, whose new **Find a day for legs** button opens the finder already set to your weakest muscle group.

### Program playlist (new in 2.43.0)

Run programs one after another with no thinking about it, for example **8 weeks of Chris Bumstead's push / pull / legs, 1 deload week, then 8 weeks of Arnold's double split**. **Programs → Playlist**:

- **Build a playlist:** add programs (search by name or athlete) and **deload weeks**, set the number of weeks for each with − and +, reorder with the arrows, remove with ✕. It shows the total, for example "17 weeks in all, about 4 months". A program you add runs for its own length (12 weeks for most of the library). Two ready-made ones are offered: **Mass cycle** (12 weeks of CBum push/pull/legs, a week off, 12 weeks of Arnold's double split) and **Base then size** (6 weeks of Reg Park's full-body 5x5, a deload week, 12 weeks of Frank Zane's upper / lower).
- **Start today** makes the first program your current program, starting at **Day 1** (its earlier progress in the program is cleared, and the app tells you so before you start).
- **It moves on by itself.** Each time you open the app, if the current stage's weeks are up, the next stage begins: the next program becomes your current program at Day 1 and a note says so. A deload stage keeps your current program and tells you to run it with the same weights and about half the sets. It only ever moves **one stage at a time**, so a long break never skips a whole program. After the last stage it says **Playlist finished**.
- **Home shows one slim row** above the work check-in: **Week 2 of 8 · Chris Bumstead: Push / Pull / Legs** and **Next: Deload week on Dec 23**. Tap it for the whole line-up with dates, a ✓ on finished stages and the current one highlighted. There you can **Skip to next stage**, add **+1 week** to the current stage, or **Stop playlist** (your current program stays as it is). If you switch to a different program on your own, the sheet offers **Make it my current program** to get back on track.
- **On Progress too:** the **History** tab's **Training year** section shows the whole chain with dates above the calendar (current stage marked, later stages with their start and end dates).
- If a program in the playlist was deleted from the phone, it is added back from the built-in list when its turn comes.
- The playlist is saved with your settings, so it is in backups. Stopping it, or never starting one, changes nothing.

### Your current program

Your **current program** is the one that Home, the side menu and **Start Workout** use. **You** choose it. The app never picks it for you, and browsing never changes it.

- On the **Programs** screen, tap **⋯** on a row and choose **Make it my current program**, or open a program and tap **Select**. The one you picked sits in the **Current program** card at the top, and its row shows a green **✓ Current** tag.
- Open a program you haven't selected and a bar says **Just looking** with a **Select** button. Inside one of its workouts, a small bar says **Not your current program** with **Select it**, which also makes that day your next one.
- While you look around, Home and the side menu keep showing your own program.
- **Switch week** counts as choosing the program, because you are starting it there.
- **Empty Workout**, **Do Again**, **Freestyle**, and the **Open** buttons after you add or create a program do not change it.
- If nothing is selected and you finish a program's workout, a note offers **Make "…" your current program?**. Nothing happens unless you tap it.
- Deleting a program you were only looking at leaves your current one alone.

### Programs that run for several weeks

- A **week bar** is pinned right under the header of the Days screen (**Week 4 of 12 · 3/64 done**, with a **Switch week** button). It stays there while you scroll the list of days.
- **Switch week** opens a grid of the weeks. Tap the one you are on: earlier weeks are marked done and **Up next** jumps there. Going back to an earlier week works the same way. Your saved workouts and PRs are never changed.
- This works for every program that is laid out week by week: the 12-week plans, Kris Gethin 12 Weeks to Muscle and Legacy 12-Week, FST-7 Big and Ripped, both Jim Stoppani 6-week programs, the programs that repeat a few days over several weeks (Kai Greene 6 weeks: days 1-6 for weeks 1-3, days 7-12 for weeks 4-6; FST-7 Level 1: weeks 1 and 3 use days 1-5, weeks 2 and 4 use days 6-10), and, since 2.48.0, almost everything else (below). For those, the plan stays on the first set of days until you finish them, then moves to the next week.
- **Longer programs (new in 2.48.0).** The programs that used to be one week on repeat now run for several weeks, so the week bar counts a real plan (**Week 6 of 12 · Build**) and the days list has headers like **Weeks 5-8 · Build**. Which weeks are real and which are ours is spelled out in each program's description:
  - **Weeks as published.** Shred with Buendia is 8 weeks (Phase 1 is weeks 1-4, Phase 2 is weeks 5-8). Arnold's Blueprint to Mass phase 1 and Blueprint to Cuts phase 2 are 4 weeks each (Cuts is now six days a week: Monday/Thursday, Tuesday/Friday and Wednesday/Saturday repeat). Frank Zane's beginner routine is 8 weeks: one set of every exercise for weeks 1-3, then two sets, as his manual says.
  - **Weeks that PulseTrack adds.** The original sources only describe one week or a few weeks, so for the rest we added a plain progression and say so in the description. In the 12-week programs, weeks 1-4 are the routine exactly as published. **Build** (weeks 5-8) adds a set to the first two lifts of each day (never above 5 sets) and two reps to the other plain lifts. **Push** (weeks 9-11) goes back to the published sets but heavier on the first two lifts (two fewer reps, 20 s more rest). **Deload** (week 12) is about 60% of the sets, so go lighter. Jay Cutler's 4-day split is 8 weeks the same way (4 as published, 3 Build, 1 Deload).
  - **Method programs are left alone.** Layne Norton's PHAT, Kris Gethin's DTP split, Super Shredded 8, Vince Gironda's 8x8 (6 weeks plus an easy week) and 10-8-6-15 (8 weeks) keep every set and rep as published, with just an easy final week. Arnold's Golden Six is the same three workouts for 12 weeks (the length is our choice), adding weight whenever you hit all the reps. Anything that is part of a method (8x8, FST-7 finishers, pyramids like 10, 8, 6, 15, drop sets, Tabata, AMRAP) is never changed in any program.
  - A day that would come out the same in a later phase is simply reused, so the days list only shows what really changes. Progress you already had is kept: a "Week 3" counter becomes week 3 of the plan.
- The **Pro Leg Days** and **Pro Upper-Body Days** collections, and programs you build or import yourself, are still a single week repeated. They have the same bar with a plain week counter, and **Switch week** asks for the week number.
- Every day is labelled like **Week 4 of 12 · Day 2**, the days list has week headers and scrolls to **Up next**, and rest days are skipped automatically.
- **Switch week** counts as choosing the program, because you are starting it there.
- History remembers which week each workout was in.

### Skipping a workout

Can't train today (a long day of work, an injury, a trip)? You don't need to delete the exercises or turn the day into a rest day.

- On the Home card tap **Skip this workout**, or tap **Skip day** at the top of the workout screen. You get one question first: **Skip this workout?**
- The day counts as done, so **Up next** moves on to the following day (and to the next week when the week is finished), the same as after finishing a workout. It works the same in the week-by-week plans and in the programs that repeat a few days.
- **Nothing is saved to History.** No volume, stats or streak days are added, and your records are never touched.
- Any sets you had already checked off for that day are cleared, so it comes back fresh. The question tells you how many.
- In the days list a skipped day says **SKIPPED** instead of **DONE**. It stays in the program, and you can still open it and do it later; finishing it makes it a normal **DONE** day.
- **Undo** stays on the screen for a few seconds after skipping and puts everything back exactly as it was.
- The buttons only show for the program you are currently on, on a day that has exercises. Rest days have their own **mark as done**, and Freestyle workouts have nothing to skip.

### Busy-season version (new in 2.35.0)

Slammed at work? Open any program's day list and tap **Busy-season version**. Pick **2, 3 or 4 days a week** and **30, 45 or 60 minutes** a workout. The app builds full-body days from the exercises that program leans on most (a squat, a hinge, a push, a pull and so on, at least three muscle groups a day) and checks each day fits your time. The preview shows the days before you save; **Create this program** adds it as a new program called "... · Busy season". Your own program is not changed, so you can switch back when the busy stretch ends. The button is hidden on Freestyle and on busy-season programs themselves.

### Make your own

- **Program builder:** create a program, add days and exercises, set sets, reps and rest.
- **Edit any program:** add, remove, swap or reorder exercises, and change sets and reps.
- **Move exercises up and down:** every exercise card on the workout screen has an up arrow and a down arrow (top right, above Swap and Delete). Tap one and the exercise changes places with the one above or below it. The card stays under your thumb, so you can keep tapping to carry it further. The arrows are greyed out at the top and bottom of the list. Sets you already typed or checked off move with their exercise. A superset (A1/A2) moves as one block, so it never gets split up; to change the order inside a superset, unlink it, move the exercise, then link it again. It works in every program, including Freestyle, and the new order is remembered. **Clear Progress** puts the program's original order back.
- **Save a finished workout as a routine** to repeat it.
- **Supersets:** two exercises done back to back. They show as A1/A2, B1/B2 and each has its own weight and reps. To make one, tap **+ Superset with next** in the gap between two exercises; tap **Superset with next · Unlink** to separate them. The rest timer waits until the last exercise of the pair.
- **Share** a program as a link, or **import** one from a link or a `.json` file.
- **Share one workout day (new in 2.19.0).** Edit a day the way you like it, then tap **Share day** on the workout screen (next to Clear Progress). It sends that day exactly as it is now, with your extra sets and swapped exercises, as a link (or a small file if it is very big). The other person opens the link, or taps **Programs → Paste a Share Link** and pastes it. A sheet shows what is inside and where it will go: by default the **same day of the same program** if they have it, otherwise the next day of their current program. They can change the program and the day, then tap **Replace Day N**. That day's exercises, name and length are swapped for yours. Their own day is kept underneath like any other edit, so **Clear Progress** on that day puts **their original day back** (exercises, name and length), and so does **Reset All Days**. A note under the day's name says it is a shared workout until then. Sets they had logged on that day are cleared when it is replaced; saved workouts in History and records are never touched. Or they can tap **Just do it once (Freestyle)** to run the workout without changing any program. On an iPhone, a link opened in Safari does not see the app on the Home Screen (they keep separate data), so the app offers to copy the link: open the Home Screen icon and use **Programs → Paste a Share Link**.

### Reset and restore

- **Clear Progress** puts a day back to exactly the program's original: added exercises and extra sets go, removed ones come back. Your History and records are never deleted.
- **Deleted a built-in program?** **Add Back a Built-in Program** restores it.

## While you train

### Logging a set

- **Set tags:** **R** regular, **W** warm-up, **D** drop set, **F** failure.
- **Previous numbers:** each set shows what you did last time.
- **Dumbbells:** enter the weight of **one** dumbbell. Your volume counts both hands.
- **Each side or total? (new in 2.38.0):** cable, machine and dumbbell exercises have a small tappable label above the weight box. A cable crossover has two stacks, a plate machine has plates on both sides, so do you type 25 or 50? Tap the label and pick **Each side** (type one stack, one side or one dumbbell, and volume counts both) or **Total** (type everything together, and volume counts it once). The label always says which one the app expects: **Each side · Reps**, **Total · Reps** or, for dumbbells, **Each hand · Reps**. Crossovers and flies show an amber **Each stack or total?** until you choose. The choice is remembered per exercise (and travels in backups), and the same note shows in Guided mode next to the weight. **25 each side and 50 total count as the same work** (500 volume at 10 reps either way). It only affects workouts you save from now on; past sessions keep how they were logged, and estimated 1RM and PRs use the number you typed. **Back to the app's default** in the same sheet undoes it. Dumbbell exercises still start as Each hand, and barbell, bodyweight and cardio exercises have no switch.
- **Bodyweight moves:** pull-ups and dips use your body weight, with a **Weighted / Assisted** switch.
- **Type 0 for bodyweight (new in 2.20.0):** on any other exercise (push-ups, sit-ups, lunges, air squats and so on), type **0** as the weight and the set counts **your body weight** as the load, the same way pull-ups and dips do. Volume, estimated 1RM, PRs, charts and the **e1RM** chip all use your weight from **Body & Photos**. The unit beside the box changes to **BW**, History shows **BW × 12**, and the suggestion says "bodyweight × 13". An **empty** weight box still counts as nothing, so only a typed 0 means bodyweight. It counts your whole body weight, not a share of it, and any number above 0 counts as exactly that weight, as before (it is not added to your body weight). If no body weight is logged yet, a note under the exercise offers to open **Body & Photos**; once you log one, old bodyweight sets are worked out again. When you hit the top of the rep range on every set, the suggestion says to add reps or hold a weight.
- **Timed sets (per-set timers new in 2.42.0):** planks, holds, hangs and carries (and any set with a target like "45s" or "40 sec") get a ⏱ button on the set. It counts up, or down from the target, and beeps. If the reps are written one entry per set, like **8-10, 8-10, 40 sec, 40 sec**, each set reads its own entry: only the two 40-second sets get a timer, and the reps box on each set shows that set's own target. On a **weighted** timed set (leg press for 40 seconds, curls for 40 seconds) the seconds are **not** written into the reps box, because they would make a fake estimated 1RM; the timer saves the seconds, tells you the time is up, and you type the reps you got. Bodyweight holds like planks still fill the reps box with the seconds.
- **Cardio:** log minutes and distance, and see your pace over time.
- **Notes:** add a note to any exercise or day.
- **Swap an exercise:** tap swap and **★ Similar exercises** lists the closest matches first, each with a short reason ("Same movement · Cable · Reverse grip"). **Everything in the list trains the same muscle** as the exercise you are looking at (the muscle is shown next to the title, for example "Side Delts · closest first"). Chest Dips only suggests chest exercises, Triceps Dips only triceps, and a barbell row never suggests a shoulder exercise. Inside that muscle the order comes from the movement, the equipment and the grip or angle, so Rope Pushdown suggests other pushdowns before it suggests a triceps machine, and a bench press suggests other flat presses before incline ones. Exercises you have done before are marked with your last weight and reps. Below the list, every exercise in that muscle group is still there.
- **Search by any name:** the exercise picker and the Library also search an exercise's other names. Type "barbell bench press" or "triceps rope pushdown" and you get Bench Press or Rope Pushdown, with "also called …" under it.
- **Form tips:** each exercise card has a **Form tips ›** link with a short checklist written for that exact exercise (set-up, the movement, the usual mistake to avoid) and a button to watch a video on YouTube. Every exercise in the library has its own, and every exercise the built-in programs use is in the library too. Exercises you create yourself use a general set of tips when one fits, since they have none of their own.
- **Form clips (new in 2.44.0):** next to **Form tips ›** each exercise card has a **🎥 Clips** button (with the count when there are some). It opens that exercise's clips and lets you **Record a clip** with the phone's camera or **Choose a video** you already filmed. The video file is saved as it is (nothing is re-encoded or uploaded); a clip filmed from an exercise card remembers the heaviest set you logged that day, so two clips can read **205 × 5** and **225 × 5**. Tap a clip to watch it with **0.25×, 0.5× and 1×** speed, **◀ frame / frame ▶** to step one frame at a time, a scrubber, an optional note, **Share** and **Delete**. **Compare two clips** plays two of them side by side with one set of controls (the scrubber follows the longer one). **⋯ More → Form clips** and **Body → Form clips** open the whole collection, grouped by exercise. Clips live on this phone only, like progress photos: **they are not in the backup file** (too big) and **Reset everything** removes them. A clip over 120 MB asks before saving and one over 400 MB is refused; film 10 to 20 seconds of one set. Some phones will not play a video in a format they cannot decode, in which case the clip saves but its picture stays blank.
- **Bar path and bar speed (new in 2.46.0):** on barbell lifts (squats, bench, deadlifts, presses, rows, cleans and the like) a **🎯 Bar path** button sits next to **🎥 Clips**. Prop the phone side-on so the plate on the end of the bar stays in the picture, then **tap the left edge of the plate and the right edge**. Choose which plate it is at the top (a 45 lb or 20 kg plate is 450 mm across; 35, 25, 10 and 5 lb plates and a custom size are in the list): the plate's real size gives the scale, so speeds come out in metres a second. A green ring follows the plate straight away, so you can check it holds before you lift. Press the red button (with an optional 5 or 10 second delay to get under the bar), lift, and press it again. The path is drawn on screen as you go and the clip is saved with the exercise. A clip you filmed earlier, or picked from your library, works too: **Form clips → open the clip → 🎯 Track bar path**, scrub to a frame where the plate is clear, tap its edges and press **Track**. **Results:** the path over the first frame (slow parts red, fast parts green, a numbered dot at the top of each rep), the **average speed**, the **best rep**, the **velocity loss** (how much slower the last rep was than the fastest) and the range of motion, then a row per rep with **up speed, peak speed, time up and front-to-back drift**, and a plain line on what the set looked like. **Share picture** makes a card of it. The numbers are saved with the clip and shown under it in the player (**View**, **Track again**). It all happens on the phone: a small tracker written for this app follows the plate frame by frame, so nothing is uploaded and no model has to be downloaded; it works offline. **Tips for good numbers:** film from the side at the height of the plate, keep the phone steady (lean it on something), use even light, and film every set from the same spot so sets can be compared. Phone cameras blur fast bars a little, so compare your own sets filmed the same way rather than other people's numbers. The tracker can lose the plate if something covers it, if it swings a long way out of the picture or if the picture is very dark; it says so and the numbers are marked as rough. A clip recorded inside the app is saved as a WebM or MP4 file, whichever the phone's browser makes.
- **Warm-up generator:** builds warm-up sets for your first lift.
- **Screen stays awake** during a workout, on phones that allow it.
- **Resume:** if you leave the app mid-workout, it picks up where you stopped.

### Say a set (rebuilt in 2.37.0)

The **🎤 Say a set** button on the workout screen fills in a set from your voice. The first version often missed, so it was rebuilt.

- **It understands how people talk in a gym:** "bench 185 for 8", "one eighty five for eight", "two plates for five", "8 reps at 135", "225 x 5", "pull ups 12", "curl forty five pounds ten reps". Phones often write "for" as "4", so "185 4 8" is read as 185 for 8.
- **It never fills a set blind.** A small panel shows what it heard, with the exercise, weight and reps ready to fix and a **Log set** button. Pick a different exercise or change a number, then log.
- **If the phone can't listen** (no internet, microphone blocked, or the iPhone Home Screen app, which does not allow speech input) it says why and lets you type the set in the same panel.

- **Limits:** most phones send your voice to the phone maker for recognition, so it needs internet and can fail in a basement gym. That is a phone limit, not something the app can fix while staying offline.

### In the gym (new in 2.8.0)

Each of these can be switched off in **Settings → Workout Screen**.

- **Suggested weight.** Under an exercise that has a rep range (like 8-12) and a last time, a small card shows what you did and what to try today, with a **Fill in** button (and an Undo). The rule is the usual one: hit the top of the range on every working set and it suggests a heavier weight; miss the bottom of the range on a set and it keeps the weight; otherwise it asks for one more rep. "Heavier" means the next weight you can really load. A barbell uses the plates from your gym profile, and dumbbells use that gym's dumbbell range, so at the heaviest dumbbell it says to add reps instead. It stays quiet when there is nothing to compare (first time, AMRAP, pyramids, timed sets).

- **Timer starts on your first set.** The workout timer shows **Ready** until you check off a set, so opening a day and getting a coffee doesn't count. Tap the pill to start it early.
- **Look over before saving.** **Finish Workout** shows a short summary first: time, sets, volume compared with the last time you did that day, any new records, and a note if some sets aren't checked off. **Keep going** or **Save workout**. This screen is also the "are you sure?" step: Finish Workout never saves straight away, and **Save workout** ignores taps for the first moment so an accidental double-tap can't save it. Turn the look-over off and Finish Workout asks a plain **Finish workout?** question instead.
- **Favorites and Recent.** In the exercise picker, tap the ☆ next to any exercise to pin it. **★ Favorites** and **Recent** sit at the top of the picker, and the ☆ is also on the exercise's page.
- **Set row options.** Choose what shows on every set: **Last time**, the **warm-up / drop tag**, and **Est. 1RM**. Weight, reps and the check button always show.
- **Left-hand mode** moves the check button and the **+ Set / − Set** buttons to the left side.

### Build me a workout (new in 2.19.0, much bigger in 2.21.0)

Home → **Build a Workout** (in the **Start training** row), or **Build a workout** in the side menu. Pick what to train, how long you have and a goal, and it builds a session for you. No account and no internet needed.

- **A different mix every time (2.21.0).** It used to hand out much the same lifts each time. Now every build is a weighted draw from the best choices for each muscle, and it steers away from what it gave you in the last few builds, from exercises you swapped away, and from lifts in your last three saved workouts. Build Push eight times and you get eight different sessions, with no big lift missing and no odd picks.
- **Variety:** **Favourites** leans on lifts you already do and the classics. **Balanced** is a bit of both. **Mix it up** (the default) goes for fresh picks. Hit **Shuffle** for another version.
- **Lock, swap, remove, add (2.21.0).** After it builds, each exercise has a **🔒** (keep it when you Shuffle: **Shuffle the rest**), a **↻** (swap just that one for another exercise for the same muscle) and a **✕** (remove it). **＋ Add an exercise** adds one more for the muscle that has the fewest. The time updates as you change it.
- **What to train:** **Pick for me**, Push, Pull, Legs, Upper body, Lower body, Full body, Chest & back, Shoulders & arms, Arms, Glutes or Core, or tap any muscles yourself (13 to choose from). **Pick for me** looks at your recovery map and this week's sets and chooses the freshest group (Push, Pull or Legs), leaves out a muscle that is still recovering, and tells you why. With no history yet it starts you on Full body.
- **How long:** 15, 20, 30, 45, 60, 75 or 90 minutes. It counts sets and rest times and lands within about 10% of the time you asked for. Short on time, it leaves out the smallest muscles and says so.
- **Goal:** **Build muscle** (big lifts 6-10 reps, isolation 10-15), **Get stronger** (big lifts 3-6 reps with longer rests) or **Tone & endurance** (12-15 and 15-20 reps with shorter rests).
- **More options (2.21.0):** **Equipment**: your gym (default), Dumbbells, Machines & cables, Barbell or Bodyweight, always inside what your active gym has. **Number of exercises**: Auto, or 3, 4, 5, 6 or 8 (if that many will not fit the time, it says how long they take). **Style**: Straight sets, or **Supersets (saves time)**, which pairs opposite muscles (chest with back, biceps with triceps) and fits more into the same time. **Extras**: a **Core finisher** adds one abs exercise at the end.
- **What it picks from:** the exercise library, using each exercise's muscle. It only offers what the **active gym** can do, starts each muscle with a big lift, never picks the same movement twice if there is another choice, favours well-known lifts, and leaves out jumps, carries, stretches and cardio. The first lift of a big muscle gets 4 sets (3 when time is tight).
- **Start workout** opens it as a normal **Freestyle** workout (supersets stay linked), so you can still swap, add, delete, reorder, **Share day** or save it as a routine. If your last Freestyle workout still has exercises it asks before replacing them. The app remembers your last choices. The **Build** and **Shuffle** buttons stay in view at the bottom while you scroll the list.

### Work check-in (new in 2.19.0)

Landscaping, building, moving house: a hard day at work uses the same back, grip and legs you lift with. Home has a one-row **Work today** card with three buttons: **Light or off**, **Normal** and **Heavy**. Tap one at the end of the day (tap it again to clear it, or tap **Yesterday?** to fill in yesterday). It is saved per day and is part of your backup.

- **Only Heavy changes anything.** A heavy day counts in the **Recovery map** as extra hard sets for lower back, forearms and grip, legs, shoulders, traps and back, so the Muscles tab, the "still recovering" line on Up next and **Pick for me** all know your back is tired. Chest and arms are not affected. Light and Normal days are just a record.
- **Suggested weight stays put.** On a heavy day, lifts for those muscles keep their weight instead of suggesting more ("Heavy work day, so keep this weight and add a rep or two"). Chest and arm lifts still go up.
- **Build me a workout goes easy.** It leaves out deadlifts, bent-over rows, shrugs and carries, prefers machines and cables, and builds one exercise fewer.
- **A heavy day reshuffles the week (new in 2.44.0).** If you mark Heavy and the workout up next is hard on those same muscles (Legs, or a back day), the Work card offers a lighter day from the same program to do today instead: *"Heavy work today, and Legs is hard on the same muscles. Chest & Arms is easier on them."* **Do Chest & Arms today** moves it up; **Keep Legs** hides the offer for today. Nothing else changes: no day is marked done, the days keep their order, Legs stays up next once you have done the swapped-in day, and **Back to Legs** puts it back with one tap. How hard a day is on you comes from the same weights as the recovery map (lower back and forearms 4, legs 3, shoulders, traps and back 2, everything else 0), averaged over the day's hard sets. A day is only offered a swap at 1.5 or more, and the day offered has to score at least 1 lower. Not offered for Freestyle, after you have already trained today, in the "Yesterday?" view, or when **Go easier after heavy work days** is off.
- **Switch:** **Settings → Workout Screen → Go easier after heavy work days** (on by default). Turn it off and the check-in only feeds the recovery map. Hide the card with **Settings → Home screen**.

### Pre-workout protocol (new in 2.45.0)

- **The steps** are two by default: **Eggs + bananas** (60 minutes to settle) and **Creatine + caffeine** (45 minutes to kick in). Tap **Taken** when you have had one and its countdown starts; **earlier…** is for something you took 5 to 45 minutes ago, **undo** for a mis-tap. When a countdown ends you get a chime, a buzz, a spoken line and a message ("Creatine and caffeine are kicking in. Head in and warm up."), plus a notification if the app is in the background and notifications are allowed.

### Guided mode (sets added in 2.36.0, each-side note in 2.38.0)

Tap **Guided mode** on the workout screen for one exercise at a time with big steppers: weight and reps, one big **Log set** button, and **Prev**, **Swap** and **Skip** at the bottom. ### Rest timer

- Starts by itself when you check off a set (you can turn this off in Settings).
- In a superset it waits until the last exercise of the round. It does not start after your final set, and it uses a short rest after warm-up sets.
- **Smart rest times:** heavy lifts get longer rests and small lifts shorter. You can set your own time for any exercise.

### Workout timer (top of the screen)

A timer starts when you open a workout. **Tap it** for the controls:

| Option | What it does |
|---|---|
| **Pause / Resume** | For a long break or a phone call. Paused time is not counted. |

### Tabata timer

An interval timer for HIIT. Open it from the side menu (**Tabata Timer**) or from a Tabata exercise in a program (**Launch Tabata Timer**).

- It beeps at 3, 2, 1 and at each change (following your Sound and Vibrate settings) and keeps the screen on, on phones that allow it. It times itself from the clock, so if your phone sleeps for a bit, it catches up to the right place when you return.

### Your own exercises

Missing a lift? Add it from the **Create your own exercise** button in the exercise picker (searching for something that doesn't exist offers it with that name), from the **＋ Create your own exercise** button in the Exercise Library, or from **My Exercises** on Home.

- **Pick the main muscle** (14 choices). It counts toward your weekly muscle volume and lights up on the muscle map like any built-in exercise. Whatever you don't pick, the list fills up with other exercises for the same muscle. You can change the picks later with **Edit**.
- **Pick how you log it:** weight × reps, dumbbells, bodyweight ± weight, cardio with distance, or timed.

### Plateau tips

A lift counts as **stalled** when you've done it **3 or more times in the last few weeks** and none of those sessions beat the best you had before. You pick how many weeks with the **2 wk / 3 wk / 4 wk / 6 wk / 8 wk** buttons on the Plateau check card (Progress → Overview); **4 weeks** to start, and your choice is remembered. Each stuck lift shows how many weeks it has been since your best, for example **6 weeks**. A lift you've stopped doing drops off by itself, and matching your best counts as no improvement. The app then works out why and suggests something that fits:

| What happened | Tip |
|---|---|
| Same weight every session | Add a rep to every set before adding weight, or take a smaller jump (2.5 lb / 1 kg). |
| Reps climbing at the same weight | Keep going. Add weight when you hit the top of your rep range. |
| Added weight but lost reps | Stay at that weight until the reps come back. |
| Down 5% or more from your best | Check sleep and food, and consider a lighter week. |
| Anything else | Try a small weight drop, a different rep range, or a similar exercise for a few weeks. |

The tip shows on the Overview tab, on the exercise card in your workout, and on the exercise's chart screen.

## Cardio and sports

Sports also have a **Result** (Win, Loss or Draw). You can log past days too.
- **Live timer:** tap **▶ Start timer**, pick the activity and go. Pause, resume or finish whenever. Finish opens the save form with the time already filled in. > 💡 **Back up now and then.** Everything lives on your phone. If you clear browser data or get a new phone, a backup file is how you keep it all.

- **Backup and Restore:** save all your data to a file (workouts, programs, body entries, your cardio and sports log and your Coach mode teams, results and gear), and load it back on this phone or a new one. Form clips, progress photos and the hype track song are big files that stay on the phone and are not in the backup.
- **Automatic safety copies (2.24.0):** the first time you open the app each day (after you have a workout saved), PulseTrack keeps a copy of everything on the phone. The newest 8 are kept. **Settings → Data & Backup → Automatic safety copies** lists them (date, number of workouts, version) with **Restore** next to each, and **Make one now**. Going back first saves a copy of how things are right now, so you can undo it. They live on the phone, so they protect against a mistake or a bad update, but clearing the browser's data removes them: a backup file is still the real safety net. The backup reminder on Home now appears after 7 days.
- **Repeat last workout (2.25.0):** **Repeat last workout** in the menu, and **Do this workout again** on the finish screen, fill today's day with the weights and reps from your most recent saved workout.
- **Undo delete (2.24.0):** deleting a workout from History or a body entry shows **Undo** for 10 seconds.
- **No storage cap:** when the normal space fills up, PulseTrack keeps saving in your phone's large storage, for as long as your phone has room. Settings shows **No size limit**.
- **CSV export:** download your workout history for Excel or Google Sheets.
- **Share a program** as a link, or **import** one.
- **Share a workout day** as a link or file (see Make your own), and **Programs → Paste a Share Link** opens a pasted link for a day or a whole program.
- **Share a workout:** after you finish, or from **History → a workout → Share**, you get a picture card (date, time, volume, sets, PRs, the muscles you worked and your best set for each lift, with a gold PR badge and how your estimated max changed). It uses your theme colours and font. **Share image** sends it to Messages, Instagram and so on, **Copy as text** pastes it into any chat, and **Save image to this phone** keeps it.

## Good to know

**Works offline.** After the first load, everything (programs, exercises, charts, styling, fonts) is stored in the app. Only the YouTube "How to do it" links and getting updates need internet.

**iPhone**
- Updates can lag. Close the app fully and reopen it online.
- If you don't use the app for a long time, iPhone may clear its stored files. Keep a backup.
- It has not been tested on a real iPhone.

**About the numbers.** Recovery times and target ranges are estimates and typical guidelines, not medical or coaching advice. Form tips are general guidance: if something hurts, stop and ask a coach or doctor.

**What a web app can't do.** Apple Health or Strava sync, a watch app, home-screen widgets, a lock-screen timer, or reminder notifications. Those need a native app or a server.

**About Kris Gethin 12 Weeks to Muscle.** The sets, reps and rest were copied from the plan day by day, with a few gaps filled in: where no rest time was given, 90 seconds is used (60 seconds for 15+ reps); "Seated Calf Raise" on day 19 has no set count, so it uses 3; day 58 is missing the name of one exercise, so it shows as **Exercise of Your Choice**; and a few exercises where the sets and the listed rep targets disagree. Each of these is noted on the exercise, including which number was used. Exercise names were matched to the PulseTrack list, and 26 new exercises were added for moves that did not exist yet.

**About the 12-week programs.** The source plans had a few gaps, handled like this:
- Ferrigno: the heading says weeks 5–8, but the schedule says weeks 7–12.
- Arnold: no phase lengths were given, so each phase is 3 weeks.
- Mountain Dog: one week repeats for 12 weeks.
- Phil Heath Phase 3: cardio has no time listed.
- Ronnie Coleman: crunches have no sets or reps listed.

## Managing the project

### Files

Upload these to your GitHub repo, all in the same folder:

| File | What it does |
|---|---|
| `index.html` | The whole app (about 1.8 MB, including fonts and themes) |
| `programs.js` | The 56 built-in programs |
| `exercises.js` | The 625 exercises, each with its own form tips and the muscle it trains |
| `sw.js` | Makes it work offline and update |
| `manifest.json` | Lets it install to your home screen |
| `README.md` | This file |

**Update steps:** upload the changed files on GitHub → open the app online → close and reopen.

### How program changes reach phones

- A program that is **new** in `programs.js` shows up on phones automatically.
- A program that **already existed** and was edited is *not* replaced on phones, so your progress is safe. To get the changed version, delete the program in the app and use **Add Back a Built-in Program**.
- Two exceptions. **Week info:** if a built-in program is still the same as when you got it but did not yet have week numbers (FST-7 Big and Ripped, Jim Stoppani Shortcut to Shred) or a week plan (Kai Greene, FST-7 Level 1), the app fills that in by itself the next time it opens. Only the missing week numbers are added; exercises, edits and history are never touched, and a program you rebuilt yourself is left alone. A "Week 3" you had typed in before becomes week 3 of the program, once. **Longer programs (2.48.0):** a built-in program that used to be one week on repeat gets its extra weeks added to your copy, as new days after the ones you have, when your copy still has its original days numbered 1 to n. Your own edits to those days stay, and nothing is replaced. If you removed or reordered days in it, it is left as it was. Rest days that an old program had stored as an exercise (USN 5 Day Mass Plan) become real rest days. **Wording:** if a built-in program still carries an old source note, the app swaps in the cleaned text by itself. It only touches text containing an old note, so anything you wrote or edited is never changed, and no exercise, set, rep or logged workout changes.

### Adding programs and exercises

- **Easiest:** build a program inside the app, or import a `.json` file with the Import button.
- **Permanent for everyone:** add it to `programs.js`, one object per program with `id`, `title`, `description`, `tags` and `days`. Each day has `dayNumber`, `title`, `estimatedMinutes` and `exercises` (each with `id`, `name`, `sets`, `reps`, `restSeconds`, `type`, `notes`). For two-a-day programs, title the sessions like `"Mon AM: Chest"` and `"Mon PM: Arms"` (or `"Chest (AM) + Arms (PM)"`) and the app adds the sun and moon look, the AM+PM tag and the Two-a-day shelf by itself; add `"Two-A-Days"` to `tags` too. For the Programs screen to show the athlete, goal and level, add `pro` (a name, or "" for none), `goal` (`"mass"`, `"cutting"`, `"strength"` or `"balanced"`), `level` (`"beginner"`, `"intermediate"` or `"advanced"`) and `perWeek` (workouts a week, or `null`). For a program that lists every week, add `week: 1…12` to every day. For a program whose few days repeat over several weeks, add `weekPlan: [{ weeks: [1, 2, 3], days: [1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6] }, { weeks: [4, 5, 6], days: [7, 8, 9, 10, 11, 12] }]` to the program itself. Both give it the pinned **Switch week** bar. An empty `exercises` list is a rest day.
- **New exercises:** use **My Exercises** in the app, or add them to `exercises.js` for everyone. One line each: `{ name: "Zercher Deadlift", category: "Back", muscle: "Lower Back" }`. `muscle` is what Similar exercises matches on (the list of muscles is at the top of `exercises.js`). Without it the app works the muscle out from the name, and only falls back to the category when it can't. If the exercise is also known by other names, list them in `aka`: `{ name: "Rope Pushdown", category: "Triceps", aka: ["Triceps Rope Pushdown"] }`. Those names are searchable and count as the same exercise (same muscle group, never suggested as a swap for itself). Only give it its own entry if it really is a different exercise (a straight-bar pushdown is not a rope pushdown). To give an exercise its own form tips, add `tips` (3 or 4 short steps) and `avoid` (the usual mistake): `{ name: "Zercher Deadlift", category: "Back", tips: ["Hold the bar in the crooks of your elbows", "Keep your chest up and your back flat", "Stand up by driving your hips forward"], avoid: "Rounding your back at the start." }`.

Keep every `id` unique. New programs reach phones on the next update.
